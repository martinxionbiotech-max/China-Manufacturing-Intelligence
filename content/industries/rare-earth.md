# China Rare Earth Industry

China's rare-earth industry anchors on Baotou (light rare earth, Bayan Obo) and Ganzhou (ionic heavy
rare earth) — the world's dominant rare-earth producer.

## Industry at a glance

- **2024 output: 270,000 tonnes, ~70% of global** (reserve share fell to 48.89%) [1].
- **Northern Rare Earth + China Rare Earth Group control 70%+ of mining quota** [1].

## National footprint

- [Baotou](/cities/baotou/): light rare earth (Nd, Pr) — Bayan Obo, the world's largest deposit.
- [Ganzhou](/cities/ganzhou/): heavy ionic rare earth (Dy, Tb) — 60%+ of national quota.
- [Ningbo](/cities/ningbo/): finished NdFeB magnets (downstream of both).

## Data deep-dive

The October 2024 *Rare Earth Management Regulation* imposed total-quantity control, full-process
traceability, and **export controls on 7 categories of medium/heavy rare earth**, directly reshaping the
global supply chain [1]. 2024's first-batch quota set mining at 135,000 tonnes and smelting/separation
at 127,000 tonnes, split between China Rare Earth Group and Northern Rare Earth [2]. Recycling is
emerging as a supply relief valve (98% leaching recovery in one molten-salt-electrolysis technique).

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Rare earth is a **strategic, quota-governed industry** — supply is policy-allocated, not
market-cleared. The element split (light vs. heavy) is the key structural fact: Baotou owns the abundant
light elements, Ganzhou the scarce heavy elements, and Ningbo the finished-magnet layer. Recycling and
the 70%+ quota concentration are the two forces shaping supply.

*Opinion:* For buyers, the critical insight is **element-matching and licensing**. High-temperature
magnets (Dy, Tb) → Ganzhou heavy rare earth; standard NdFeB → Baotou light feedstock; finished magnets →
Ningbo. Export controls make licensing and end-use verification the binding constraint, not price.

## Buyer notes

- Light rare earth (Nd, Pr) → [Baotou](/cities/baotou/).
- Heavy rare earth (Dy, Tb) → [Ganzhou](/cities/ganzhou/).
- Finished NdFeB magnets → [Ningbo](/cities/ningbo/).
- Export controls apply — verify licensing and end-use.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, rare earth is a strategic, quota-governed industry — supply is policy-allocated, not market-cleared — and the light-versus-heavy element split is the key structural fact.*

For high-temperature magnets (dysprosium, terbium), route to Ganzhou's heavy rare earth; for standard NdFeB, route to Baotou's light feedstock. Element-matching and licensing, not price, are the real skills.
