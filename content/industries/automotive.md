# China Automobile Industry

China's automobile industry anchors on Changchun (FAW, the cradle) and Shanghai (Tesla's Gigafactory) —
the world's largest auto market for 16 straight years.

## Industry at a glance

- **2024 production and sales: 31.282 million units — #1 globally for 16 consecutive years** [1].
- **NEVs: 12.888 million produced (+34.4%); 40.9% of total new-car sales** (up 9.3 pts) [2].
- **Exports: 5.859 million units (+19.3%)** [2].

## National footprint

- [Changchun](/cities/changchun/) is the **domestic-brand pole** (FAW/Hongqi, 1,034 parts firms).
- [Shanghai](/cities/shanghai/) is the **export-grade pole** (Tesla Gigafactory).
- [Shenzhen](/cities/shenzhen/) produced **2.93 million NEVs (23% of national)** — 1 in 5 Chinese NEVs
  [3].

## Data deep-dive

The NEV split is shifting: battery-electric fell to 60% of NEV sales (down 10.4 pts) while plug-in
hybrids rose to 40% (up 10.4 pts) [2]. Chinese-brand passenger cars reached 65.2% market share in 2024
(+9.2 pts) [1]. This is a structural story: domestic brands + hybrids are now the growth engine, not
foreign-joint-venture sedans.

## Original analysis

*Inference:* China's auto industry is a **dual-pole + distributed assembly** structure — Changchun
(domestic brand) and Shanghai (foreign/export) as the strategic poles, with Shenzhen emerging as the
volume NEV producer (23% of national). The hybrid surge (40% of NEVs) signals the market is optimizing
for range/refueling, not pure electrification.

*Opinion:* For buyers, match the **brand tier and compliance requirement** to the city: export/compliance
NEVs → Shanghai; domestic-brand cost structures → Changchun; volume EV/electronics-integrated NEVs →
Shenzhen. The hybrid shift matters for component sourcing — expect dual powertrain (engine + electric)
demand to persist.

## Buyer notes

- Export/compliance NEVs → [Shanghai](/cities/shanghai/) (Tesla ecosystem).
- Domestic-brand NEVs → [Changchun](/cities/changchun/) (FAW/Hongqi).
- Volume NEVs → [Shenzhen](/cities/shenzhen/) (23% of national).
- Plan for hybrid (not just pure-EV) component demand.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, China's auto industry is a dual-pole plus distributed-assembly structure — and matching brand tier and compliance requirement to the city is the whole sourcing game.*

Export and compliance NEVs go to Shanghai; domestic-brand cost structures go to Changchun; volume EV and electronics go to Shenzhen. Treat the three as different tiers of the same industry, not interchangeable regions.

*This is my editorial judgment, not a verified fact — the sourced figures are above.*
