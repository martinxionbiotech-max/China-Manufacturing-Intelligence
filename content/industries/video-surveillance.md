# China Video Surveillance Industry

China's video-surveillance industry is anchored by Hangzhou's Binjiang district — home to Hikvision (海康
威视) and Dahua (大华股份), which together hold ~65% of the market.

## Industry at a glance

- Hikvision and Dahua hold a combined **~65% market share** [cls-hangzhou-digital-security].
- **Global video-surveillance market: ~US$69.63 billion (2023); China = 40–50% of global camera sales**
  [1].

## National footprint

Hangzhou is the camera-and-platform anchor; Shenzhen is the complementary component-and-manufacturing
node.

## Data deep-dive

China is the world's largest professional video-surveillance camera producer, with 40–50% of global
sales [1]. Hikvision posted RMB 41.2 billion H1-2024 revenue (+9.68%) and Dahua RMB 14.867 billion
(+1.59%) [cls-hangzhou-digital-security]. Both are pivoting from pure surveillance to AIoT — a structural
shift from "cameras" to "intelligent sensing platforms."

## Original analysis

*Inference:* This is a **duopoly cluster** — two firms hold ~65% share from one district. It is the
mirror image of the fragmented category-monopoly clusters (Guzhen lighting, Chenghai toys): here,
concentration is extreme, and the value is shifting from hardware to AIoT software. China's 40–50% global
camera share is the volume base beneath the duopoly.

*Opinion:* For buyers, the practical implication is that video surveillance is a **two-vendor category** —
most enterprise procurement lands on Hikvision or Dahua. The key decision is now the AIoT software layer
(analytics, platform) rather than the camera hardware, which is commoditizing. Buyers should evaluate the
platform/analytics stack as the differentiator, not megapixels.

## Buyer notes

- Cameras and surveillance platforms → Hangzhou (Hikvision, Dahua, ~65% share).
- Evaluate the AIoT/analytics software layer as the real differentiator, not camera hardware.
- Shenzhen is the component/manufacturing complement, not the brand anchor.

## The Author's Take
