# China Hardware & Tools Industry

China's hardware-and-tools industry spans Yongkang (power tools, doors) and Yangjiang (kitchen cutlery) —
two complementary category monopolies.

## Industry at a glance

- **2024 domestic hardware-tools market: RMB 103.96 billion** [1].
- **Yongkang: 5 of every 10 global power tools, 7 of 10 Chinese doors, 6 of 10 thermos cups**
  [cls-yongkang-hardware].
- **Yangjiang: 7 of every 10 Chinese kitchen knives** [cls-yangjiang-knives].

## National footprint

- [Yongkang](/cities/jinhua/): power tools, doors, thermos (RMB 50B+ output).
- [Yangjiang](/cities/yangjiang/): kitchen cutlery (2,000+ makers, standards-led).

## Data deep-dive

The two clusters divide the category by niche: Yongkang owns *powered and structural* hardware
(electric tools, doors), Yangjiang owns *cutting* hardware (knives, scissors). Yongkang's power-tool
chain is 50 years old with 70%+ local parts rate; Yangjiang leads 1 international + 3 national standards
— owning the quality-definition layer of its niche [cls-yongkang-hardware][cls-yangjiang-knives].

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Hardware is a **niche-split industry** — no single city owns all of "hardware"; instead
Yongkang and Yangjiang each hold a category monopoly on opposite sides of China. Both are enterprise-density
monopolies, not brand monopolies, which keeps pricing competitive but quality variable across the tail.

*Opinion:* For buyers, anchor **power tools and doors → Yongkang** (per-factory safety certs required) and
**kitchen cutlery → Yangjiang** (standards-led, safer starting point). Match the specialist to the
category rather than seeking "the" dominant brand.

## Buyer notes

## The Author's Take

*In my view, hardware is a niche-split industry — no single city owns all of "hardware," and Yongkang and Yangjiang each hold a category monopoly on opposite sides of the country.*

So anchor power tools and doors to Yongkang (with per-factory safety certification as a hard gate), and kitchen cutlery to Yangjiang (where standards leadership matters more). The two are complements, not competitors.
