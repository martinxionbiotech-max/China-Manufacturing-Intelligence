# China Textiles & Garments Industry

China's textile industry spans Keqiao (1/4 of world fabrics), Hangzhou Bay (1/5 of national), and Zhuji
(70% of socks) — the world's largest textile-and-garment system.

## Industry at a glance

- **2024 above-scale textile revenue +4%; profit +7.5%** across 38,000 firms [1].
- **229 textile-and-garment clusters (27 provinces) hold 302,000+ firms and RMB 4.18 trillion output**
  [2].

## National footprint

- [Shaoxing](/cities/shaoxing/): fabric/dyeing core (Keqiao, ~40% of national dyeing).
- [Hangzhou Bay](/cities/hangzhou/): garment layer.
- [Nantong](/cities/nantong/): home textiles (Dieshiqiao).
- [Zhuji/Datang](/cities/shaoxing/): socks (70% of national).

## Data deep-dive

Capacity utilization is strong: **textiles 78.5%, chemical fiber 85.4% (2024), both above the 75%
national industrial average** [1]. The 229-cluster structure is the sector's backbone — 302,000+ firms
and RMB 4.18 trillion output, with an "east leads, center supports, west rises, northeast specializes"
regional pattern [2].

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Textiles is a **layered, cluster-distributed industry** — fabric (Shaoxing), garments
(Hangzhou Bay), home textiles (Nantong), and socks (Zhuji) each anchor a distinct layer, unified by the
Hangzhou Bay and SWT cluster designations. The 229-cluster count shows a sector organized around
geographic specialization rather than single champions.

*Opinion:* For buyers, route by layer: **raw/printed fabric and dyeing → Shaoxing (Keqiao)**;
**garments → Hangzhou Bay**; **bedding/home textiles → Nantong**; **socks → Zhuji**. The above-average
capacity utilization signals healthy demand — expect competitive but not distressed pricing.

## Buyer notes

## The Author's Take

*In my view, textiles is a layered, cluster-distributed industry — fabric, garments, home textiles, and socks each anchor a distinct geography, so "textile" is four sourcing decisions wearing one name.*

Route by layer: raw and printed fabric plus dyeing to Shaoxing (Keqiao); garments to Hangzhou Bay; home textiles to Nantong; socks to Zhuji. Match the layer to the cluster, not the label to one city.
