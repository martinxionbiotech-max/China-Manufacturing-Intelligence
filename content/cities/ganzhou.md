# Manufacturing in Ganzhou

Ganzhou anchors China's heavy ionic rare earth — the dysprosium and terbium that heat-resistant magnets
and aerospace alloys cannot do without. It also hosts Jiangxi's only national rare-earth advanced
manufacturing cluster.

## City role

Ganzhou is the **heavy-rare-earth capital**, complementing [Baotou](/cities/baotou/)'s light-rare-earth
(neodymium, praseodymium) pole. The two cities divide the rare-earth world by *element type*, not by
competition.

## What the city makes

Ganzhou controls 60%+ of China's ionic rare-earth quota production and rare-earth-oxide supply
[cls-ganzhou-rare-earth]. It targets a "1+5+N" cluster architecture — one RMB 500B+ cluster, five RMB
200B+, N RMB 50B+ — with China Rare Earth Group's modern-chain projects launching in Ganxian district.

## Data deep-dive

- **Rare-earth new-materials & applications cluster** — Jiangxi's 3rd national advanced manufacturing
  cluster [1].
- **Nonferrous-metals cluster output > RMB 200 billion** [2].
- **New-energy & NEV chain target: RMB 250 billion by 2026** (lithium battery RMB 200B) [2].
- **Xinfeng county electronics: RMB 28.34 billion (2025), 75% of its above-scale industrial value** [3].

## Cross-cluster position

Ganzhou's heavy rare earth feeds the same downstream magnet chain as Baotou's light rare earth — both
flow into [Ningbo's magnetic-materials cluster](/cities/ningbo/) and the global NdFeB-magnet industry.
The difference is *which element*: Ganzhou supplies the heat-resistant heavy elements.

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Ganzhou owns the **scarcest layer** of the rare-earth chain — heavy ionic rare earth is
geologically rarer and more concentrated than light rare earth. Its 60%+ quota share makes it the
price-setting node for dysprosium and terbium globally. The electronics and NEV-battery diversification
(RMB 250B target) is a deliberate hedge against rare-earth price cycles.

*Opinion:* For buyers, the critical insight is **element-matching**. High-temperature magnets (EV motors,
wind turbines, aerospace) need heavy rare earth → Ganzhou. Standard NdFeB magnets need light rare earth →
Baotou feedstock. Both are export-controlled; verify licensing and end-use, and expect allocation
(not price) to be the binding constraint on heavy elements.

## Buyer notes

- Heavy rare earth (Dy, Tb) for high-temp magnets → Ganzhou (60%+ quota).
- Light rare earth (Nd, Pr) → [Baotou](/cities/baotou/).
- Finished magnets → [Ningbo](/cities/ningbo/).
- Export controls apply; verify licensing and end-use.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Ganzhou owns the scarcest layer of the rare-earth chain — heavy ionic rare earth is geologically rarer and more concentrated than light rare earth, and its dysprosium and terbium are irreplaceable for heat-resistant magnets.*

For high-temperature magnets (EV motors, wind turbines, aerospace), you need Ganzhou's heavy rare earth. Standard NdFeB routes to Baotou instead. Element-matching is the whole game.
