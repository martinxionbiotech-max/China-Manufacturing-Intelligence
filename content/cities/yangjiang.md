# Manufacturing in Yangjiang

Yangjiang anchors China's kitchen-cutlery industry — 7 of every 10 Chinese knives.

## City role

Yangjiang is the **cutlery category monopoly** — a single-niche city, complementing Yongkang's hardware
(power tools, doors) on the other side of China.

## What the city makes

Yangjiang has 2,000+ cutlery manufacturers, 8,500+ operators, and 5,000+ product types, exporting to
130+ countries — 7 of every 10 Chinese knives, at ~RMB 10 billion annual output [cls-yangjiang-knives].
It leads or participates in 1 international, 3 national, 8 industry, 5 local, and 4 alliance standards
for knives and scissors.

## Data deep-dive

- **Industrial investment +63.5% (2024), #1 in Guangdong; manufacturing investment +32.3%** [1].
- **"4+4" pillar industries** under the "industrial revitalization" program [1].
- **Expanding into high-end aluminum/titanium/copper/magnesium alloys and new energy storage** [2].

## Cross-cluster position

Yangjiang and [Yongkang](/cities/jinhua/) are **complementary hardware niches**: Yangjiang owns kitchen
cutlery; Yongkang owns power tools, doors, and thermos. Both are category monopolies, not brand
monopolies.

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Yangjiang is a **category monopoly, not a brand monopoly** — it dominates kitchen cutlery
through enterprise density (2,000+ makers), not a single champion. Its standard-setting leadership (1
international + 3 national standards) signals it owns the *quality-definition* layer, not just volume.
The #1-in-province industrial-investment growth (+63.5%) shows a city aggressively diversifying beyond a
single niche.

*Opinion:* For buyers, Yangjiang is the correct anchor for **kitchen cutlery**, and its standard-setting
makes it the safer starting point than Yongkang's power tools (which need per-factory safety certs). But
value still comes from the long tail of specialized makers, not "the" dominant brand — match specialist
to category.
