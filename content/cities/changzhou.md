# Manufacturing in Changzhou

Changzhou anchors China's carbon-fiber and composites industry — a "chokepoint-breaking" material that
secures the supply chain for large aircraft and aerospace. It is also a rising new-energy city whose
2025 new-energy output passed RMB 1 trillion.

## City role

Changzhou is the **carbon-fiber pole** of China's advanced-materials industry, complementing Ningbo's
magnetic-materials pole.

## What the city makes

Changzhou has built the complete carbon-fiber chain — "precursor production → composite forming →
equipment manufacturing → end products" — with the new carbon-materials cluster surpassing RMB 120
billion, more than a third of Jiangsu's provincial total [cls-changzhou-carbon-fiber]. The anchor firm
is Sinofibers (中简科技).

## Data deep-dive

- **New-energy industry scale: RMB 1.0479 trillion (2025)** — a new city card [1].
- **New-energy vehicle output > 800,000 units; transformer output = 13% of national** [1].
- **Power-battery industrial chain completeness: 97%** (materials → cells → systems → testing) [1].
- **National single champions: 18 → 45; national "little-giants": 12 → 315** over the "14th Five-Year"
  period [1].

## Cross-cluster position

Carbon fiber is listed in China's 2024 *Key New Materials First-Batch Application Demonstration
Catalog*, explicitly framed as core to aircraft and aerospace supply-chain security
[cls-changzhou-carbon-fiber]. Its downstream applications — wind power, hydrogen, aerospace — connect
Changzhou to Yancheng's wind cluster and the broader new-materials sector.

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Changzhou's RMB 120B "new carbon materials" figure is broader than carbon fiber alone — it
spans graphite and advanced-carbon materials. The carbon-fiber-specific chain is the aerospace-critical
core, while the wider cluster is a materials ecosystem. Its RMB 1-trillion new-energy base (with 97%
battery-chain completeness) is a second, equally large industrial identity.

*Opinion:* For buyers, Changzhou is the correct anchor for **aerospace-grade carbon fiber and
composites**, but it carries export-control sensitivity (aircraft end-use). Verify end-use licensing up
front. For new-energy batteries/vehicles, it is a credible near-complete-chain source. For commodity
composites, the wider Jiangsu new-materials belt offers more price-competitive options.

## Buyer notes

- Aerospace-grade carbon fiber and composites → Changzhou (Sinofibers).
- New-energy batteries (97% chain completeness) and transformers → Changzhou.
- Export controls apply to aerospace grades — verify end-use.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Changzhou's RMB 120B "new carbon materials" figure is broader than carbon fiber alone — it spans graphite and advanced-carbon materials — and the carbon-fiber-specific story is a chokepoint-breaking one.*

For aerospace-grade carbon fiber and composites, Changzhou is the correct anchor, but it carries export-control sensitivity around aircraft end-use. Verify end-use, and do not read the headline number as carbon fiber alone.
