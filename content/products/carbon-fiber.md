# Where Is Carbon Fiber Made in China? Changzhou's Composites Cluster

Changzhou (常州), Jiangsu, anchors China's carbon-fiber and composites industry — a strategic material
that secures the aerospace supply chain.

## Product at a glance

- **HS code:** 6815 (articles of stone, incl. carbon-fiber articles).
- **Anchor cluster:** Changzhou carbon fiber cluster (`cls-changzhou-carbon-fiber`).
- **Model:** chokepoint-breaking strategic material + complete chain.

## The cluster, in data

Changzhou has built the complete carbon-fiber chain — precursor, composite forming, equipment, and end
products — with the new carbon-materials cluster past RMB 120 billion (over 1/3 of Jiangsu's total)
[cls-changzhou-carbon-fiber]. The anchor firm is Sinofibers (中简科技).

## Market data

China's new-materials industry reached an estimated **RMB 8.48 trillion output (2024)**, targeting RMB
10 trillion by 2025 [1]. Carbon fiber is on the national *Key New Materials First-Batch Application
Demonstration Catalog* (2024), explicitly framed as core to aircraft and aerospace supply-chain
security [1][2].

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Carbon fiber is a **strategic, export-controlled material** — its value is supply-chain
security (aerospace) more than commodity volume. The RMB 120B "new carbon materials" figure is broader
than carbon fiber alone (it spans graphite and advanced-carbon), so treat the carbon-fiber-specific core
as the aerospace-critical subset.

*Opinion:* For buyers, Changzhou is the correct anchor for **aerospace-grade carbon fiber and
composites** (Sinofibers), but export controls apply to aircraft end-use — verify end-use licensing up
front. For commodity composites, the wider Jiangsu new-materials belt offers more price-competitive
options.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, carbon fiber is a strategic, export-controlled material, so its value is supply-chain security, not commodity volume — and that changes what "sourcing" means here.*

Changzhou is the correct anchor for aerospace-grade fiber and composites, but export controls attach to aircraft end-use, so verifying end-use is a hard requirement, not a formality.
