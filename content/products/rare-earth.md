# Where Is Rare Earth Sourced in China? Baotou's Bayan Obo and Ganzhou

Baotou's Bayan Obo is the world's largest rare-earth mine, anchoring China's rare-earth chain with
Ganzhou (ionic clay).

## Product at a glance

- **HS code:** 2805 (alkali/alkaline-earth metals; rare-earth metals).
- **Anchor clusters:** Baotou rare earth (`cls-baotou-rare-earth`), Ganzhou rare earth
  (`cls-ganzhou-rare-earth`).
- **Model:** light (Baotou) + heavy (Ganzhou) dual source.

## The cluster, in data

Baotou's Bayan Obo is the world's largest rare-earth deposit (light elements Nd/Pr); Ganzhou's ionic
clay holds China's heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) [cls-baotou-rare-earth][cls-ganzhou-rare-earth]. Together
they anchor ~70% of global supply.

## Market data

China produced **270,000 tonnes of rare earth (2024) — ~70% of global, with reserve share at 48.89%**
[1]. The October 2024 *Rare Earth Management Regulation* imposed total-quantity control and **export
controls on 7 categories of medium/heavy rare earth** [1][2].

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Rare earth is a **quota-governed, dual-source industry** — the light/heavy element split
(Baotou vs. Ganzhou) is the defining structural fact. Heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) is the scarce,
export-controlled layer; light rare earth (Nd/Pr) is abundant. This is a policy-allocated supply, not a
market-cleared one.

*Opinion:* For buyers, the practical guide is element-matching: **light rare earth (Nd/Pr) → Baotou**;
**heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) → Ganzhou**; finished magnets → Ningbo. Export controls and licensing are the
binding constraint, not price.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, rare earth is a quota-governed, dual-source industry, and the light-versus-heavy element split is the defining structural fact you must match against.*

Light rare earth (Nd/Pr) routes to Baotou; heavy rare earth (Dy/Tb) routes to Ganzhou. High-temperature magnets cannot substitute around the heavy fraction, so element-matching — and licensing — is the real skill.
