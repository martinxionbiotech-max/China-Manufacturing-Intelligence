# Where Are Security Cameras Made in China? Hangzhou, Home to Hikvision and Dahua

Hangzhou's Binjiang district anchors China's video surveillance — Hikvision (海康威视) and Dahua (大华
股份) hold ~65% combined share.

## Product at a glance

- **HS code:** 8525 (transmission apparatus for TV; cameras incl. digital).
- **Anchor cluster:** Hangzhou digital security (`cls-hangzhou-digital-security`).
- **Model:** duopoly cluster.

## The cluster, in data

Hikvision and Dahua hold a combined **~65% market share** from Hangzhou's Binjiang district
[cls-hangzhou-digital-security]. Hikvision posted RMB 41.2 billion H1-2024 revenue (+9.68%); Dahua RMB
14. 867 billion (+1.59%).

## Market data

**2023 global video-surveillance market: ~US$69.63 billion; China = 40–50% of global camera sales** [1].
Both anchors are pivoting from pure surveillance to AIoT — a shift from "cameras" to "intelligent
sensing platforms."

Consumer-grade online sales hit **25.69 million cameras (2024, +10.4%)**, showing the category's shift toward smart-home channels [2].

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Security cameras are a **duopoly cluster** — two firms hold ~65% share from one district,
the mirror image of fragmented category-monopoly clusters. The value is migrating from camera hardware
(commoditizing) to the AIoT software layer (analytics, platform).

*Opinion:* For buyers, this is effectively a **two-vendor category** — most enterprise procurement lands
on Hikvision or Dahua. The real decision is now the AIoT/analytics stack, not megapixels. Evaluate the
platform layer as the differentiator; Shenzhen is the component/manufacturing complement.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, security cameras are a duopoly cluster, and that means this is effectively a two-vendor category — most enterprise procurement lands on Hikvision or Dahua.*

The real decision has already moved past the camera itself to the AIoT and analytics stack. So the sourcing question is less "which camera maker" and more "which platform you are committing to."
