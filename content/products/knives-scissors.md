# Where Are Knives Made in China? Yangjiang

Yangjiang (阳江), Guangdong, is China's cutlery capital — "中国刀剪之都" — the source of **7 of every
10 knives** made in China.

## Product at a glance

- **HS codes:** 8211 (knives with cutting blades), 8213 (scissors and blades).
- **Anchor cluster:** Yangjiang knives cluster (`cls-yangjiang-knives`).
- **Model:** single-niche category monopoly + standards leadership.

## The cluster, in data

Yangjiang holds **2,000+ knife-and-scissors manufacturers**, **8,500+ related operators**, and
**5,000+ product types**, selling to **130+ countries** at nearly **RMB 10 billion** annual output
[cls-yangjiang-knives]. The "7 of 10 knives" figure is the headline. Its standards leadership is the
under-reported story: Yangjiang leads or participates in **1 international, 3 national, 8 industry, 5
local, and 4 alliance standards** for knives and scissors.

## Market data

Yangjiang's cutlery cluster is the concentrated core of China's hardware/cutlery export base. The
**whole-chain scale exceeds RMB 55 billion**, with 25 enterprises above RMB 100 million in output value
[1]. Its share of China's production is **~70% of national output** and **~85% of national exports**, sold
to 160+ countries and regions [1]. The e-commerce layer is compounding: 2024 domestic e-commerce sales
reached RMB 9.93 billion (+12.6%) and cross-border platform transactions RMB 2.93 billion (+10.9%) [2].
Its standards moat (1 international + 3 national standards) makes it the quality-definition node, not
just a commodity-volume node.

## Original analysis

*Inference:* Yangjiang is a **category monopoly with a standards moat** — unlike a pure commodity
cluster, it owns the *quality-definition* layer (1 international + 3 national standards). That is rare
for a consumer-hardware cluster and signals the industry consolidated *upward* into standards rather
than *outward* into brands.

*Opinion:* For buyers, Yangjiang is the correct anchor for **kitchen cutlery**, and its standards
leadership makes it the safer starting point than commodity alternatives. But note the asymmetry: it
dominates *kitchen* cutlery, not industrial blades — match specialist to category. The 7-of-10 share is
an industry estimate; treat as magnitude.
