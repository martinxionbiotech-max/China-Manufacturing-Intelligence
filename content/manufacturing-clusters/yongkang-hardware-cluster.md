# Yongkang Hardware: China's Hardware Capital in Central Zhejiang

Yongkang — a county-level city under Jinhua in central Zhejiang — is the fourteenth formation mechanism
in this project. It is the manufacturing twin of nearby Yiwu: where Yiwu moves small commodities through
trade, Yongkang makes the hardware — power tools, doors, and vacuum cups — that flows through that
network.

## What the cluster is

Yongkang is officially billed as "China's Hardware Capital" (中国五金之都) and, in its own
promotional framing, the "World Hardware Capital" [2]. The hardware industry accounts for **90% of
the city's industrial output**, with Jinhua's hardware (power-tool) sector above **RMB 50 billion**
[1]. Its market — the China Science & Technology Hardware City — is the **nation's largest and the
world's third-largest** hardware market, with **RMB 45.532 billion** turnover in 2022 [1]. The
share numbers are the headline: **7 of every 10 Chinese doors**, **5 of every 10 power tools
globally**, and **6 of every 10 vacuum cups** come from Yongkang [1].
## The anchor firms behind the categories

Industry media name a recognizable tier of local champions by category [1]:

- **Doors** — Wangli (王力), Buyang (步阳), Qunsheng (群升): the security-door tier behind the
  "7 of 10 Chinese doors" claim.
- **Power tools** — Boda (博大), Sanfeng (三锋): the power-tool tier.
- **Vacuum cups** — Haers (哈尔斯): the listed vacuum-flask maker that anchors the "6 of 10
  vacuum cups" category.
- **Diversified hardware groups** — Xingyue (星月) and others spanning motorbike parts to doors.

This named tier is the useful buying map: each category has one or two flagship firms plus a long
tail of workshops; certification and quality follow the same split.

## Market context: a mid-size global category

Yongkang's niches sit inside a growing but modest global market. Industry reports put the global
hardware-tools market at roughly **US$35 billion in 2024** (US$32B in 2023, ~6.5–7% CAGR
2020–2024), with China as the largest producer and roughly **45–50% of global exports** [3]. The
Chinese domestic hardware-tools market was about **RMB 103.96 billion in 2024**, with research
houses forecasting roughly **RMB 167.34 billion by 2029** [4][5]. Jinhua's policy goal — announced
at a 2024 power-tool industry-chain meeting — is to build the power-tool cluster into a **RMB 100
billion** industry [6]. Read these as market-research and policy calibers, not census.

*Inference:* the mechanism is "multi-niche category monopoly + distribution moat." Yongkang does not own
one category; it owns three or four simultaneously (doors, power tools, vacuum cups). That breadth
suggests a shared *mechanism* (metalworking + stamping + assembly skill) rather than niche-specific
advantage. Its market (RMB 45.5B turnover) is a *distribution* moat as much as a manufacturing one.

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Yongkang is a **multi-niche category monopoly** — its breadth (doors + power tools + vacuum
cups) is the unusual feature, signaling a shared metalworking mechanism rather than niche depth. For
buyers, Yongkang is the correct anchor for **commodity hardware and power tools**, with the market
(RMB 45.5B) as the discovery floor. But the category breadth is a double edge: volume is unmatched,
while per-factory quality and safety certification vary widely — demand per-model CE/UL certification
for power tools. The Yiwu-Yongkang pairing (trade + manufacturing) is the structural insight: the two
counties form one trade-and-make machine in central Zhejiang.

## What it makes

- **Power tools, hand tools, doors, vacuum cups.**
- **Commodity hardware** across multiple niches.
- **Anchor firms by category**: doors (Wangli, Buyang, Qunsheng), power tools (Boda, Sanfeng),
  vacuum cups (Haers), diversified groups (Xingyue).

## Jinhua context: the city's ten industrial chains

Yongkang sits inside Jinhua's restructured industrial strategy. In 2024 the city formalized **ten
key industrial chains** — new-energy vehicles and key components, smart photovoltaics and new
energy storage, robotics, and others — and designated **62 "chain-leader" enterprises** to pull
suppliers along each chain [7]. Jinhua's above-scale industrial output reached **RMB 509.27
billion in 2024**, with core-optoelectronics and NEV components together ~40.8% of it [8].

The hardware cluster is not at the center of Jinhua's new-energy push, and that is the point:
Yongkang's value is as the **mature, self-organized counterpart** to the city's younger strategic
chains — a demand-and-export-proven base whose tools and doors feed construction and
manufacturing globally rather than a policy-nurtured rising sector. Buyers assessing supply
stability should note the difference: Yongkang's chain survived decades without chain-leader
designation; the new-energy chains are being built with it.

## The technology shift: materials and precision replace brute metalworking

Yongkang's commodity reputation obscures a quiet upgrade. New alloys and polymer materials are
raising wear resistance, corrosion resistance, and strength across tool lines, while CNC
machining and 3D printing are replacing stamping-and-grinding for complex, fine-tolerance
products [10]. The pattern: the cluster's metalworking *skill* remains the base, but the
*materials and precision* layers are being added on top — the same trajectory that moved
Shunde's appliances and Yueqing's electrical from copied goods to certified exports.

The practical consequence for buyers: "Yongkang commodity" is no longer a single quality
class. The top tier (anchor firms like Boda or Sanfeng) now competes on materials and
precision; the long tail still sells the old commodity standard. Qualify by tier, not by town
name.

## The market-plus-factory layer: a discovery floor, not just a trading hall

Yongkang's China Science & Technology Hardware City is the cluster's physical discovery
engine — RMB 45.5 billion in 2022 turnover means the market itself is a comparable economic
unit to the city's factories [1]. The pattern matches Lecong (furniture) and Yiwu (small
commodities): a deep trading floor glued to a factory base turns sourcing into a two-step
process — survey the market for category and spec, then qualify the factory behind the
stall. For hardware specifically, the market solves the catalog problem that no single
supplier can: doors, power tools, vacuum cups, and assorted hardware in one physical place.

The honest reading: the trading floor is also why the 10,000-firm figure is fuzzy. The
market concentrates not just manufacturers but traders and assemblers, so cluster-firm
counts mix production and distribution layers. Treat "10,000 firms" as the size of the
ecosystem, not the factory census.

## The honest caveats

- **Industry estimates.** "5/10 power tools" etc. are estimates, not census.
- **Certification variance.** Quality and safety certs vary across the tail.
- **Report calibers.** Global market estimates range from ~US$35B (narrow tools) to ~US$127B
  (broad hardware) depending on product scope [3][9]; domestic figures likewise.

## What this means for buyers

- **Commodity hardware and power tools** at volume.
- **Market (RMB 45.5B)** = discovery floor.
- **Export readiness** — China supplies 45–50% of global hardware-tool exports [3].
- **Use the anchor-firm tier as the quality ceiling** of each category, then qualify down the
  tail case by case.
- **Tier by tier, not by town** — the materials-and-precision upgrade sits in the anchor tier;
  the commodity standard survives in the tail [10].
- **Market-first workflow** — survey the Hardware City for category and spec, then qualify the
  factory behind the stall [1].
- **Caveats:** demand per-model CE/UL certification; treat share figures as magnitude.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Yongkang is a multi-niche category monopoly — its breadth across doors, power tools, and vacuum cups is the unusual feature, signaling a shared metalworking mechanism rather than a single niche.*

That breadth means one city can serve several hardware categories at once, which is convenient. But breadth also means you should verify per-category depth rather than assuming mastery across all of them.
