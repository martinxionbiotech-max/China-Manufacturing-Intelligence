# Foshan Ceramics Cluster: China's Tile Capital, Now a Brand and Standard Hub

Foshan, in Guangdong's Pearl River Delta, is where modern Chinese building ceramics began. In
1983 a tile factory in its Shiwan district imported Italy's first glazed-tile production line,
and China's modern ceramic tile industry was born [1]. Four decades later, Foshan is no longer
just the country's biggest tile producer — it is the industry's brand, standard, and machinery
headquarters.

For a buyer of ceramic tiles, sanitary ware, or tile-making equipment, Foshan is the cluster that
sets the rules and supplies both the product and the machines that make it.

## What the cluster is

Foshan's ceramics industry passed **RMB 100 billion in output value in 2023** (RMB 101.97B), with
tile output of 745 million m² [2]. Its position:

- Foshan's tile output has long held **30–40% of China's national total** (peaking near 50% in
  2006), and its 2023 tile exports of RMB 11.45B were roughly a third of the national total [2][3].
- The city hosts **8,000+ registered ceramics entities and ~2,000 brands**, plus 152 high-tech
  firms and eight listed companies including Dongpeng, Keda, Monalisa, and Arrow [2].
- Foshan produces ~**80% of China's ceramic presses and 85% of polished-tile presses** (Lital,
  Keda), making it the world's primary source of tile-making machinery [4].

## Origin: from Shiwan's 5,000-year kilns to a modern industry

Foshan's ceramics heritage runs deep — Shiwan (Stone Bay) has over 5,000 years of pottery history.
The modern industry's turning point came in 1983 with that first Italian glazed-tile line, then in
1991 the Shiwan-Nanzhuang bridge opened capital to Nanzhuang, which became "China's No.1 building
ceramics town." In 2002 China Ceramics City opened, shifting the cluster from factory-floor selling
to branded marketing [1].

*Inference:* this is the ninth formation mechanism in this project, and it is the clearest
"manufacturing-flight plus brand-headquarters" case. Starting in 2007, Foshan deliberately moved
kilns out — to Qingyuan, Heyuan, Zhaoqing, Guangxi, and Jiangxi — under an industrial upgrade
program. The production left; the brand, headquarters, R&D, standards, marketing, and machinery
stayed. Foshan transformed from a volume production base into a **value-retention hub** [2][5].

## The "Foshan Ceramics" collective brand

After 15 years and three rejections, Foshan finally registered the "Foshan Ceramics" collective
trademark in 2020 — a signal that the cluster now competes on provenance and standards, not just
price [2]. Leading firms are now leading the industry's quality-grading push: Monalisa was the
first to certify its full product line to the new 5A quality standard (GB/T 45817-2025) and holds
1,121 authorized patents, including the world's first ammonia-hydrogen zero-carbon ceramic firing
line [6][7].

## The honest caveats

- **A strong cyclical downturn.** National tile output has fallen since its 2014 peak of 10.6
  billion m². Foshan's H1 2024 output fell 9.8% year-on-year and exports fell 23.3%, with kiln
  utilization at only 52% — driven by China's property downturn and anti-dumping duties from 42
  countries [2][5].
- **Production has moved out.** Buyers who assume "Made in Foshan" should verify where the actual
  kiln is located; Foshan is increasingly a brand and machinery center, not the firing site.
- **Fragmented industry.** No single tile firm holds even 10% of the national market; it remains a
  "big industry, small companies" sector, which affects consistency and bargaining [5].

## What this means for buyers

- **Brands and standards:** Foshan is where China's strongest tile brands (Dongpeng, Monalisa,
  New Pearl, Eagle) and the industry's quality standards are set — the right place to source
  higher-tier, brand-certified products.
- **Rock slabs and innovation:** Foshan leads the rock-slab (岩板) category and functional,
  large-format ceramic panels, the fastest-growing high-value segments.
- **Tile-making machinery:** for buyers building or upgrading a plant, Foshan is the world's
  primary source of presses and kilns (~80% of China's presses).
- **Caveats:** confirm the actual production location and the supplier's certification track
  record; and price through the current cyclical trough rather than against peak-cycle pricing.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Foshan is the clearest manufacturing-flight-plus-brand-headquarters case in this project, and it reframes what you are actually buying.*

Since 2007 Foshan deliberately moved kilns out to Qingyuan, Guangxi, and Jiangxi while keeping brand, HQ, R&D, and standards at home. So "Foshan tile" increasingly means Foshan-owned and Foshan-specified — if you want the cheapest kiln output, you may be buying from the relocation cities, not Foshan itself.
