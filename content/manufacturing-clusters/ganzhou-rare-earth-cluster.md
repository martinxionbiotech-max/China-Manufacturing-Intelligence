# Ganzhou Rare Earth: China's Heavy Rare Earth Stronghold

Ganzhou is the twenty-sixth formation mechanism in this project — a resource-endowment cluster built on
scarce heavy rare earths, consolidated by a central SOE and extended downstream into new materials.

## What the cluster is

Ganzhou (赣州), Jiangxi, controls **60%+ of China's ion-adsorption (ionic-type) rare-earth production
quota and oxide supply** [1]. It targets an internationally influential rare-earth new-materials and
applications cluster under a **"1+5+N" plan** (1 cluster >RMB 500B, 5 >RMB 200B, N >RMB 50B) [2]. The
**China Rare Earth Group** — a central SOE — anchored its chain-strengthening project in Ganzian
district [3].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "resource endowment + central-SOE consolidation + downstream extension."
Ganzhou owns the *heavy* rare earth fraction (ionic clay — dysprosium, terbium), the rarer,
higher-value, more strategically-sensitive ore than Baotou's light rare earth. The central-SOE
consolidation (China Rare Earth Group) is the structural story: policy is concentrating the heavy rare
earth chain into one vertically-integrated champion.

## Data deep-dive

- **60%+ of China's ionic rare-earth quota and oxide supply** [1].
- **"1+5+N" plan** — a >RMB 500B anchor cluster plus downstream tiers [2].
- **China Rare Earth Group** — central-SOE chain consolidation [3].

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Ganzhou is the **heavy-rare-earth anchor** — complementary to Baotou's light-rare-earth
anchor. Heavy rare earth (dysprosium, terbium) is what gives NdFeB magnets high-temperature performance,
so Ganzhou's 60%+ ionic quota matters *disproportionately* to magnet buyers (Ningbo). For buyers, this
is a **quota-regulated market** — price and availability track policy as much as geology. The "1+5+N"
plan is a *target*, not a result; discount forward-looking scale. The central-SOE consolidation is a
signal of tightening strategic control.

## What it makes

- **Rare-earth magnets** — the downstream extension.

## What this means for buyers

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Ganzhou is the heavy-rare-earth anchor, and its scarcest output — dysprosium and terbium — is what gives NdFeB magnets their high-temperature performance.*

That is the strategic point: heavy rare earths are the rarer fraction and the one that actually constrains high-end magnet supply. Ganzhou's central-SOE consolidation and downstream extension make it the anchor for that critical fraction, complementary to Baotou's volume ore.
