# Jinjiang Footwear: China's Sports Shoe Capital

Jinjiang — a county-level city under Quanzhou in Fujian's Minnan region — is the fifteenth formation
mechanism in this project. It shares the "private grassroots reverse engineering" DNA of Wenzhou and
Shunde, but it is the rare county economy that climbed the full ladder: from OEM subcontracting, to
self-branding, to reverse-acquiring global brands.

## What the cluster is

Chendai (陈埭) town — under **40 km²** — holds **3,000+ shoe firms** producing **1 billion+ sports shoes
a year**, making Jinjiang "China's Shoe Capital" [1]. Jinjiang's shoe-and-garment industry first crossed
**RMB 300 billion** in 2022, with the textile sector alone above RMB 100 billion [1]. Anta (安踏) posted
**RMB 53.651 billion** revenue in 2022, ranking #1 among Chinese sportswear firms [1]. The cluster
produced champions — Anta, Xtep (特步), 361° — whose supply chains are overwhelmingly local: R&D,
design, and component sourcing resolve within a **50 km radius** [1].

## City-level scale: Quanzhou's RMB 700 billion textile-shoe industry

Jinjiang is the manufacturing core of a larger Quanzhou system. In 2024 Quanzhou's textile-shoe
industry passed **RMB 700 billion** in annual output value, with **sports-shoe production at 40% of
China's total and 20% of the world's** [3]. The same year, the **Quanzhou modern sports-products
cluster** — leisure sports as its signature, textile-shoes at its core, fused with sports
equipment, leather goods, and sports services — entered the national advanced manufacturing
cluster list [4]. Fujian's 2024 county-industry white paper counts Quanzhou's textile-garment
cluster among five province-level clusters each above RMB 300 billion [5].

## The brand ladder and the standards play

Jinjiang's trajectory runs OEM → self-brand → global-brand ownership (Anta's acquisitions in the
Fila/Amer Sports complex) [1]. The ladder is still moving in 2024: Anta led the formation of a
**sports-goods industry innovation consortium**, pulling suppliers, universities, and research
institutes into joint innovation and participation in global standard-setting [6]. The
second-tier brands are growing on the same base: Xtep's H1-2024 revenue was **RMB 7.2 billion
(+10.4%)** and 361°'s was **RMB 5.141 billion (+19.25%)** [3].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "grassroots reverse engineering + brand ladder + 50-km radius." Jinjiang
climbed the full ladder — OEM → self-brand → reverse-acquiring global brands (Anta's Filo/Amer Sports) —
a rare county-economy trajectory. The 50-km supply radius is the structural moat: brands iterate fast
because their entire supply chain is a day-trip away.

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Jinjiang is a **brand-anchored density cluster** — unlike Guzhen's anonymous commodity floor,
Jinjiang's density (3,000 firms in 40 km²) is organized *behind* homegrown brands. For buyers, Jinjiang
is the correct anchor for **performance and fashion sneakers**, and the 1-billion-pair figure signals
unmatched capacity. But the cluster's edge is *speed and brand co-design*, not raw low cost — commodity
white-label sneakers may be cheaper elsewhere. The "reverse-acquiring global brands" (Anta's Filo
acquisition) is the structural signal: Jinjiang is climbing the value chain through *brand ownership*,
not just manufacturing.

## What it makes

- **Sports shoes and running shoes.**
- **Soles and shoe materials** — the upstream layer.
- **Sports apparel and equipment** — via the wider Quanzhou cluster.

## The industrial ecology: big fish lead, small fish swim

Jinjiang's density is organized by Quanzhou's deliberate "big hand pulls small hand" (大手拉小手)
ecosystem policy: in a trillion-yuan city economy, leading enterprises and SMEs extend the
industrial chain together — the city formally designates industrial leading enterprises (32 in
Quanzhou's Taiwan-investment zone alone in 2024) and cultivates 专精特新 (specialized, refined,
differentiated, innovative) firms across nine industries [7][8].

For buyers this matters in one specific way: the branded tier is not a closed club. Second-tier
and component suppliers are actively groomed into the champions' supply chains, which is why
Xtep and 361° can grow at 10–19% off the same local base. The ecosystem policy lowers the risk
of sourcing from the tier just below the champions — these firms are institutionally tied into
brand quality systems.

## Market context: sports footwear is a US$100B+ global category

Jinjiang's champions compete in a large, growing category. The global sports-footwear market
was about **US$116.2 billion in 2025**, forecast to reach US$190.87 billion by 2034 (5.76%
CAGR) [9]. China remains the world's largest shoemaking center — its shoe output holds half of
global production — and produced roughly **13.5 billion pairs in 2023**, while shoe *imports*
stay small (190–200 million pairs a year), confirming China's role as net exporter of
export-grade footwear [10].

For Jinjiang the market math is: the cluster's 1 billion sports shoes a year serve a global
sports-footwear market that grows mid-single-digits annually, with the growth concentrated in
performance and athleisure — precisely the segments where the Anta/Xtep/361° brand ladder
competes. The structural risk is the same as the opportunity: the category's premium is
brand-captured, so Jinjiang's returns track its brands' ability to hold global share, not its
factories' cost position. The cluster's answer — visible in the Anta smart park and the
consortium — is to keep investing in the brand-and-R&D layer that captures that premium [6][11].

## The innovation front: smart factories and 50+ listed companies

Jinjiang's upgrade is physical, not rhetorical. Anta's Jinjiang smart industrial park (Chidian
town, 206 mu, RMB 1.4 billion investment, started July 2024) packs a dozen-plus smart
production lines and new R&D infrastructure — a global sports-science lab and a global
innovation center [11]. The town hosts **50+ listed companies** in total [11]. The
product-level view matches: a single sports shoe involves 100+ processes from yarn and
fabric through sole to finished product, and the industry now treats each process as a
technology front, not a labor cost [12].

The takeaway for buyers is concrete: the "brand ladder + 50-km radius" story now has a
capital-and-R&D layer. Jinjiang's top tier is no longer a fast-follower OEM base; it is
building the lab infrastructure of a global sportswear capital. Xtep's H1-2024 net profit
(RMB 752M, +13%) and 361°'s (RMB 790M, +12.23%) show the second tier converting revenue
growth into profit [10].

## The honest caveats

- **Brand-anchored.** Value tracks Anta/Xtep/361°, not commodity cost.
- **County-level.** Jinjiang is within Quanzhou; the two overlap.
- **Cluster-scope figures.** The RMB 700B figure is Quanzhou-wide (textile + shoe), not
  Jinjiang-only; the 40%/20% sports-shoe share is city-caliber reporting.
- **Revenue years.** Anta's RMB 53.65B figure is 2022; the brand ladder moves fast — re-verify
  current financials for procurement decisions.
- **Market calibers.** Global market-size figures are research-house estimates, not official
  statistics.

## What this means for buyers

- **Performance/fashion sneakers** at 1B-pair capacity.
- **Brand co-design + 50-km supply radius** is the differentiator.
- **Standards-tracked ecosystem** — the Anta-led consortium is pulling the supply base into
  global standard participation; useful for compliance-sensitive programs.
- **Capital-and-R&D layer** — Anta's smart park (global sports-science lab, innovation center)
  means the top tier can support co-development, not just production [11].
- **Caveats:** not the low-cost floor; pair with Quanzhou sports cluster.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Jinjiang is a brand-anchored density cluster, and that brand layer is what separates it from an anonymous commodity floor like Guzhen.*

Its 3,000 firms in a tight radius are organized behind homegrown brands (Anta and its peers) rather than behind anonymous price. For branded or brand-capable footwear sourcing, that structure matters; for pure commodity volume, the calculus is different.
