# Dongguan Smart Mobile Terminal Cluster: One in Five Smartphones, One Town

Dongguan is the second formation mechanism in this project — brand spillover from Shenzhen turned into a smartphone-building density unmatched anywhere.

Dongguan answers a different sourcing question than Shenzhen. Shenzhen is where electronics are
designed, traded, and financed. Dongguan — a city wedged between Shenzhen and Guangzhou — is where
smartphones are actually built, at a density that no other city on earth matches. If you source a
smartphone, a wearable, or a lithium battery cell, the practical geography you need to understand
is a 30-kilometer radius around two places: Chang'an Town and Songshan Lake.

## What the cluster is

Dongguan's smart mobile terminal cluster was designated a national advanced manufacturing cluster
by China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology in 2019, and ranked fourth on the 2023
China Top-100 Industrial Clusters list [1].

The scale is documented at three levels:

- In 2022, Dongguan's electronics designated-scale output reached **RMB 947 billion** [1].
- In 2023, electronics manufacturing output passed **RMB 1.1 trillion**, roughly one-quarter of
  Guangdong province's total [2].
- In 2022, Dongguan produced **196 million smartphones** — one in six phones made in China [1].
  By 2024, its smartphone shipments reached roughly **24% of the global total**: one in five
  phones worldwide, and one in eight from Chang'an Town alone [3].

The density underneath is the point. Dongguan holds some 220,000 industrial enterprises, of which
about 14,000 are above designated scale [2].

## The two-part core: Chang'an and Songshan Lake

The cluster is not spread evenly. It concentrates in a small geographic core [4].

**Chang'an Town** — roughly 80 km², population ~800,000 — sits at Dongguan's southern tip, five
minutes from Shenzhen by road. It is the historical home of the BBK Electronics lineage and today
hosts both OPPO and vivo. In 2025 Chang'an's GDP reached RMB 110 billion (its second consecutive
year as a "100-billion town"), with electronics output above RMB 200 billion — over three-quarters
of the town's industrial output [3].

**Songshan Lake High-tech Zone** — Huawei's "Ox Horn Campus" R&D base and its terminal HQ — anchors
a second pole, alongside research platforms such as the Songshan Lake Materials Laboratory [2][5].

**Binhai Bay New District**, further south, is the newer carrier: vivo's global headquarters moved
there, and it hosts OPPO's intelligent manufacturing center [6].

*Inference:* The two-pole structure — Chang'an (manufacturing) + Songshan Lake (R&D/HQ) — is
synthesized from the city government's three-year action plan and investment-promotion materials.
Chang'an's manufacturing role is strongly primary-sourced; Songshan Lake's R&D role is also
documented, but treat the exact split as directional.

## Key manufacturers

The cluster is anchored by three owned brands rather than foreign contract work [1][5][6]:

- **Huawei Terminal** (moved to Dongguan ~2015; Ox Horn Campus at Songshan Lake)
- **OPPO** (Chang'an; intelligent manufacturing center at Binhai Bay)
- **vivo** (Chang'an origins; global HQ now at Binhai Bay)

Behind them sits a dense tier of suppliers and specialists:

- **ATL (Amperex Technology)** — lithium cells powering more than half the world's smartphones,
  drones, and tablets [7]
- **Luxshare Precision** — components and assembly
- **Shengyi Technology** — PCBs and copper-clad laminates
- **Hua Bei Electronics** — contract manufacturing

The presence of three owned brands — not just factories — is what distinguishes Dongguan from a
pure OEM city. Brand ownership keeps design, software, and procurement decisions inside the same
radius as production. In 2023, the combined output value of Huawei, OPPO, and vivo grew **13.3%**,
reversing three consecutive years of decline [9] — the clearest sign that the city's pivot from
contract manufacturing to owned brands had paid off.

## The transformation path: Nokia OEM → three brands → AI phones

Dongguan's current structure only makes sense through its lineage. In the feature-phone era the
city was a Nokia contract-manufacturing base; in the smartphone era the BBK lineage (OPPO/vivo)
and Huawei Terminal turned it into a three-own-brand city with self-built factories; and 2024
opened the "AI phone" cycle, with the same brands competing on on-device AI [7]. The
"world factory → world smart-manufacturing" arc is unusually legible here because each stage left
physical infrastructure — factory campuses, supply chains, and labor pools — that the next stage
reused.

*Opinion (labeled):* Dongguan is the project's clearest **"brand + contract" dual-track** case:
unlike Shenzhen (design/trading-heavy) and unlike pure-OEM cities, three own brands and
contract manufacturers (Luxshare, Hua Bei) operate side by side on the same supply base.

## Supply-chain density: the real moat

Multiple sources describe an unusually tight supply radius. The city government states that 90% of
smartphone components can be sourced within a one-hour supply-chain radius [2]. Around OPPO's
Chang'an industrial park, roughly 67 supporting suppliers sit within 5 km; across a 30-km radius
centered on Chang'an and Songshan Lake, more than 300 core supporting enterprises cluster, with a
reported local sourcing rate above 85% [3][8].

*Caveat:* these are government and media figures, directionally credible but promotional in
nature — the "2-hour delivery" claim in particular should be read as an anecdote, not a
contractual fact. What is not in dispute is the structural point: component supply, assembly, and
brand operations coexist in an unusually small area, which compresses lead times and iteration
cycles compared with a dispersed supply chain.

## Why Dongguan formed here

*Our view:* Dongguan's cluster is best understood as a Shenzhen-adjacent manufacturing spillover.
Chang'an is five minutes from Shenzhen's border, and as of 2020 some 60 of its above-scale
industrial firms had migrated from Shenzhen, concentrated in hardware/molds and electronics [3].
The sequence runs: Shenzhen's rising land and labor costs pushed manufacturing outward, Dongguan's
proximity absorbed it, and the BBK lineage (OPPO/vivo) then compounded the effect by keeping brand,
design, and production co-located. The result is a cluster that competes on cost and speed relative
to Shenzhen, while retaining direct road access to Shenzhen's trading and financial infrastructure.

## What this means for buyers

- **Smartphones and wearables:** Dongguan is arguably the world's single densest sourcing point for
  finished smartphones, smart watches, and consumer terminals, with three owned brands plus a
  tiered supplier base.
- **Battery cells:** ATL's presence makes Dongguan a practical origin for consumer lithium cells.
- **Components, PCBs, camera modules:** the Chang'an supply radius supports component-level and
  module-level sourcing with short lead times.
- **Design-to-manufacturing:** brand + factory co-location enables fast iteration for ODM/OEM
  programs.
- **Advanced wafer fabrication:** not a Dongguan strength (as with Shenzhen, the Yangtze Delta
  leads); Dongguan's semiconductor push is oriented to packaging/testing and third-generation
  (GaN/SiC) materials [1].

## Data deep-dive

- **2022 electronics output RMB 947B** (value-added RMB 167.34B); 2023 RMB 1.1T (~1/4 of Guangdong) [1][2].
- **Huawei+OPPO+vivo combined output value +13.3% in 2023** — reversing three straight years of decline [9].
- **196M smartphones in 2022** (1 in 6 China); ~24% of global by 2024 — 1 in 5 worldwide, 1 in 8 from Chang'an alone [1][3].
- **Chang'an Town 2025 GDP RMB 110B**; electronics output RMB 200B+ (3/4 of the town) [3].
- **~220,000 industrial enterprises**, 14,000 above scale [2].
- **90% of smartphone components within a 1-hour supply radius; 85%+ local sourcing** [2][3][8].

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Dongguan's smart-terminal cluster is best understood as a Shenzhen-adjacent manufacturing spillover — three owned brands anchoring manufacturing and R&D that spilled across the border, not foreign contract work.*

The two-pole structure — Chang'an for manufacturing, Songshan Lake for R&D and headquarters — plus ATL's cell supply for over half the world's smartphones is what makes it a terminal-assembly anchor rather than a component cluster. That distinction, not the city label, is what buyers should act on.
