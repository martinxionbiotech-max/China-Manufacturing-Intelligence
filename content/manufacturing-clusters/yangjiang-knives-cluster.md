# Yangjiang Knives: Seven of Every Ten Chinese Blades

Yangjiang is the forty-ninth formation mechanism in this project — a grassroots manufacturing cluster
that makes 7 of every 10 Chinese blades and holds standard-setting authority.

## What the cluster is

Yangjiang (阳江), Guangdong, holds **2,000+ knife-and-scissors manufacturers**, **8,500+ related
operators**, and **5,000+ product types**, selling to **130+ countries** at nearly **RMB 10 billion**
annual output [1]. The "7 of every 10 Chinese knives" figure is the headline [1][2]. Its standards
leadership is the under-reported story: Yangjiang leads or participates in **1 international, 3
national, 8 industry, 5 local, and 4 alliance standards** for knives and scissors [1].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "category monopoly + standards moat." Yangjiang owns the
*quality-definition* layer (1 international + 3 national standards) — rare for a consumer-hardware
cluster — signaling the industry consolidated *upward* into standards rather than *outward* into brands.
A 30+ -process blade production line is the manufacturing depth behind it [2].

## Data deep-dive

- **7 of 10 Chinese knives** — the category-monopoly share [1][2].
- **2,000+ manufacturers / 5,000+ product types / 130+ countries** [1].
- **1 international + 3 national + 8 industry standards** — the standards moat [1].

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Yangjiang is a **category monopoly with a standards moat** — unlike a pure commodity cluster,
it owns the *definition* of blade quality, which makes it the safer starting point than commodity
alternatives. But the asymmetry matters: it dominates *kitchen* cutlery, not industrial blades. For
buyers, Yangjiang is the correct anchor for **kitchen cutlery**, with standards leadership as the
quality signal. The "7 of 10" share and "RMB 10B" output are industry estimates — treat as magnitude.
The structural lesson is that standards-setting, not just volume, is how a commodity cluster defends a
category against lower-cost entrants.
