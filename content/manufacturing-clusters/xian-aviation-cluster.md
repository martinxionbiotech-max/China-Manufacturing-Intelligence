# Xi'an Aviation: China's Cradle of Large Aircraft

Xi'an is the thirty-ninth formation mechanism in this project — a state-SOE-anchored aviation cluster
that holds China's only complete aviation industry chain and a quarter of national aerospace R&D.

## What the cluster is

Xi'an is the cradle of China's large-aircraft program — **30+ aircraft types including the Y-20 (运20)**
flew from here [1]. It gathers **~1/4 of China's aviation research-and-production strength**; the
**Yanliang (阎良) aviation base is China's only complete aviation-chain location** [2]. 2023 aviation
output was **RMB 35.8 billion**, Yanliang's 2024 total exceeded **RMB 40 billion**, and the city targets
**RMB 280 billion** aerospace scale by the end of the 14th Five-Year Plan [2][3].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "state SOE + complete chain + large-aircraft strategy." Xi'an is a
*national-strategy* cluster — its value is strategic (large aircraft, Y-20) rather than commercial,
which is why its aviation output (RMB 35.8B) is small next to consumer clusters but its strategic weight
is disproportionate. It connects to Changzhou's aerospace carbon fiber (material) and Zhuzhou's small
aero-engines (propulsion).

## Data deep-dive

- **30+ aircraft types, including Y-20** [1].
- **~1/4 of national aerospace R&D; only complete aviation chain (Yanliang)** [2].
- **RMB 35.8B (2023) → RMB 40B+ (2024, Yanliang); RMB 280B target** [2][3].

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Xi'an is the **least buyer-facing cluster in this dataset** — its value is strategic (large
aircraft), so general commercial buyers will find little here. The actionable commercial entry points
are *aerospace-adjacent*: structures, materials (Changzhou carbon fiber), and testing via the strategic
channel. The RMB 280B target vs. RMB 35.8B realized (2023) is a large plan-vs-reality gap — discount
forward-looking scale heavily. The structural insight is Xi'an's *strategic* weight: ~1/4 of national
aerospace R&D in one city is a concentration unmatched in China's civil economy.

## What it makes

- **Aircraft airframes** and aero-components.
- **Aviation R&D** — ~1/4 of national strength.

## The honest caveats

- **Strategic, not commercial.** Value is large-aircraft strategy, not commodity output.
- **Plan-vs-reality gap.** RMB 280B target vs. RMB 35.8B (2023) realized.

## What this means for buyers

- **Aerospace-adjacent supply** via strategic channel.
- **Downstream links:** Changzhou (carbon fiber), Zhuzhou (aero-engines).
- **Caveats:** not a commodity sourcing floor; discount forward-looking scale.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Xi'an is the least buyer-facing cluster in this dataset, and a general commercial buyer will find very little here — its value is strategic, not transactional.*

It holds China's only complete aviation industry chain and a quarter of national aerospace R&D, but that is large-aircraft and military-adjacent work. The actionable commercial angle is narrow, in aerospace supply-chain niches, not general sourcing.
