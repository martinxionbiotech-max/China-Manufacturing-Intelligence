# Wenzhou Low-Voltage Electrical: Liushi, China's Electrical Capital

The low-voltage electrical cluster of Wenzhou — centered on Liushi town in Yueqing — is the twelfth
formation mechanism in this project, and the purest expression of the "private chain leader + grassroots
reverse engineering" model that also shaped Shunde's home-appliance industry. No state-owned enterprise,
foreign investor, or national-base designation sat at its origin; it grew from private entrepreneurs
copying, improving, and undercutting imported switchgear.

## What the cluster is

Yueqing's electrical cluster entered MIIT's national advanced-manufacturing list in **2022** (one of 20,
with Zhejiang holding 5 seats and a RMB 10 million central fiscal subsidy attached) [1]. It is China's
**largest self-grown** low-voltage cluster and the region with the **most complete electrical-industry
chain** nationally, holding the "China Electrical Capital" and "National New Industrialization
Demonstration Base (electrical equipment)" titles [1]. It covers **200+ series, 6,000+ categories,
25,000+ models**, holding **65%+ national market share** [1]. Chint Group (正泰) was founded in 1984 by
Nan Cunhui (南存辉) and reached **US$22.1 billion** revenue in 2024 [2].

## The Liushi town story: from repair stalls to an electrical capital

The cluster's geographic heart is Liushi town (柳市镇) in Yueqing. Its origin story is the classic
Wenzhou pattern: township repairmen and traders who began by copying imported switchgear, then
improving it and undercutting it — no state anchor, no foreign investor, no national-base
designation at the start [1]. From those stalls grew the champion tier (Chint, Delixi) and a long
tail of thousands of firms, which together hold the 65% national share collectively — not through
any single firm.

## Trade context: a rebounding global category

Low-voltage electrical is a large, recovering export category. China's 2025 low-voltage trade (broad
caliber: 8 product types, 13 HS codes) totaled **US$37.81 billion in imports+exports, up 10.6%**
year-on-year, with the trade surplus widening significantly [3]. The global low-voltage market was
about **RMB 428.4 billion in 2024 (+4.1%)**, with demand rotating toward new-energy grid,
electrification, and data-center applications [4]. For Yueqing, this means its 25,000-model breadth
is pointed at the right market: grid modernization and electrification are the growth vectors, not
legacy switchgear alone.

## City context: Wenzhou's trillion-yuan private economy

Yueqing's cluster sits inside Wenzhou, the birthplace of China's private economy, which joined the
**trillion-yuan-GDP club in 2025** (the country's 28th member) [5]. The city's registered private
enterprises and individual businesses numbered 1.564 million by November 2025 [5]. The structural
point for buyers: the Wenzhou model scales through a dense private-entrepreneur base — which is
both the source of Yueqing's breadth and the reason certification discipline varies across the
tail.

*Inference:* the mechanism is "private chain leader + grassroots reverse engineering." Yueqing emerged
from township enterprises and traders — not a state anchor — which explains both its extreme category
breadth (25,000+ models) and its "one champion (Chint) + long tail" structure. The 65% share is held
*collectively* across thousands of firms, not by Chint alone.

## The domestic market structure: a channel market with rising new-energy demand

On the domestic side, China's low-voltage market was about **RMB 84.7 billion in 2023**, with
the top firms at RMB 13.14B (15.5% share) and RMB 12.8B (15.1%) — a moderately concentrated,
channel-driven structure [6]. 2024 was expected to grow only 0–1%, with distribution appliances
(配电电器) near 50% of the mix and growing fastest; **55% of low-voltage product sales in 2024
flowed through channel markets** rather than direct OEM contracts [6][7]. The bright spots are
structural: data-center/超算/智算 builds and overseas markets are growing explosively, pulling
the whole category up [7].

For Yueqing this maps cleanly onto its firm structure: the channel market rewards breadth and
the long tail (thousands of firms selling 25,000+ models through distributors), while the
data-center and export segments reward the certified champion tier (Chint-grade products). The
cluster serves both ends of the same market from one town — which is exactly what its
"champion + long tail" structure was built for.

## The national map: low voltage here, high voltage there

Yueqing's complementary pole is Baoding (Hebei) — China's northern base for power and
new-energy high-end equipment. Baoding's cluster, centered on its high-tech zone, gathers
**6,300+ enterprises** and posted **RMB 166 billion output in 2024**, with a plan targeting
RMB 250 billion by 2025 and eight firms above RMB 10 billion in main revenue [9][10]. Baoding
also ranks in the 2024 national top-100 private-economy clusters [10].

The division of labor is unusually clean for a two-cluster industry: **Baoding makes the
high-voltage and grid-scale end (transformers, transmission, wind-power equipment); Yueqing
makes the low-voltage and terminal end (breakers, contactors, relays)** [9]. A full electrical
BOM genuinely spans both. For procurement this means one industry, two travel plans — and a
reminder that "China's electrical capital" (Yueqing) and "China's electric valley" (Baoding)
are different things.

## The private-economy substrate: why breadth is the moat

Yueqing's 25,000-model breadth is downstream of Wenzhou's economic DNA. As China's private-
economy birthplace, Wenzhou's registered private enterprises and individual businesses
numbered 1.564 million by November 2025, and the city entered the trillion-yuan GDP club
the same year [5]. The Wenzhou model scales by replication: entrepreneurs copy a proven
product, split into suppliers, and the ecosystem — not any single firm — accumulates the
catalog. That is why the 65% national share is held collectively across thousands of firms
[1].

For buyers the practical rule follows: Yueqing's catalog is the broadest precisely because
no single firm could justify it. The champion tier (Chint) exists because the tail exists
— they share suppliers, workers, and standards bodies. Source by tier: the champion for
certified, bankable products; the tail for breadth and custom runs. The same logic applies
to the data-center/export boom: those segments reward the certified champion tier, while
the channel market keeps the tail alive [7].

## The honest caveats

- **Certification variance.** Chint-tier vs. long-tail quality differs sharply.
- **Industry estimates.** 65% share is an industry estimate.

  cluster's own exports.
