# Hangzhou Bay Textiles: China's #1 Trillion-Yuan Fabric Chain

The Hangzhou Bay modern textile and garment cluster is the thirtieth formation mechanism in this
project — a full-chain, trillion-yuan fabric hub anchored on upstream dyeing and the world's largest
textile distribution center.

## What the cluster is

The Hangzhou Bay modern textile-and-garment cluster's 2023 output was **RMB 1.0135 trillion — ~1/5 of
national, ranked #1** — and it entered the national advanced-manufacturing list in late 2024 [1][2].
Keqiao (柯桥) is the manufacturing core: its 910 above-scale textile/dyeing firms posted RMB 140.6
billion in 2024 (dyeing RMB 63.2B, +12.3%) [1]. Keqiao's China Textile City is the world's largest
fabric distribution center [3].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "full-chain + trillion-yuan scale + distribution hub." Hangzhou Bay is
the *manufacturing* layer of China's fabric industry — RMB 1.01 trillion output, ~1/5 national — while
Keqiao's China Textile City is the *distribution* layer. They overlap and serve different buyer
functions: production vs. discovery.

## Data deep-dive

- **RMB 1.0135 trillion (2023)** — ~1/5 national, #1 [1][2].
- **RMB 140.6 billion (2024)** — Keqiao's 910 above-scale firms [1].
- **Dyeing RMB 63.2B (+12.3%)** — the upstream finishing layer [1].
- **World's largest textile distribution center** — Keqiao [3].

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Hangzhou Bay is the **scale-manufacturing anchor** for fabric, with Keqiao as the
discovery/sampling layer (its world's-largest distribution center is the liquidity signal). For buyers,
**Keqiao = discovery, sampling, small-to-mid orders; Hangzhou Bay = scale production**. The ~40%
national dyeing share (in Keqiao) means the cluster also anchors the *finishing* step — a buyer can
source grey fabric, dyeing, and trading in one geography. The RMB 1.01T vs. RMB 140.6B (Keqiao) figures
are different scopes (cluster vs. district) — do not conflate.

## What it makes

- **Fabric** and printed textiles.
- **Apparel** — the downstream link.
- **Dyeing/printing** — ~40% national capacity.

## The honest caveats

- **Different scopes.** RMB 1.01T (cluster) vs. RMB 140.6B (Keqiao district).
- **Distribution vs. production.** Keqiao trades; Hangzhou Bay manufactures.

## What this means for buyers

- **Scale fabric production** (Hangzhou Bay) + discovery/sampling (Keqiao).
- **Grey fabric → dyeing → trading in one geography.**
- **Caveats:** match buyer function (discovery vs. production) to the right layer.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Hangzhou Bay is the scale-manufacturing anchor for fabric, with Keqiao as its discovery layer — the two are one system, not two competitors.*

The RMB 1.01 trillion output and roughly a fifth of national fabric output make it the manufacturing floor, while Keqiao's world's-largest distribution center is the liquidity signal. A fabric buyer should treat them as a single sourcing corridor.

*This is my editorial judgment, not a verified fact — the sourced figures are above.*
