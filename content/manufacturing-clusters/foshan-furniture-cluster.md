# Foshan Furniture: Lecong's Trading Hub and Longjiang's Factories

Foshan's furniture cluster — spanning Lecong and Longjiang towns in Shunde District — is the sixteenth
formation mechanism in this project: a "manufacturing plus trading-city" dual-core structure. Longjiang
makes the furniture; Lecong sells it through one of China's largest furniture trade malls, giving buyers
a single place to see, compare, and order at scale.

## What the cluster is

Shunde holds **30,000+ furniture-related firms** — over 9,000 manufacturers, 20,000+ furniture trading
businesses, and 270+ above-scale industrial firms [1]. The **Lecong Furniture City spans 2 million+ m²**,
hosting 3,000+ domestic and overseas dealers for wholesale *and* retail [1][2]. The single largest
furniture firm's output tops out near RMB 1 billion — a signal of how atomized the sector remains [1].

## The geography of Lecong: five malls along Foshan Avenue

The trading city dates to the early 1980s and grew along Foshan Avenue (佛山大道) into five anchor
malls [1]:

- **Shunlian North Zone (顺联北区)** and **Shunlian South Zone (顺联南区)** — the bookends of the
  wholesale belt.
- **Louvre (罗浮宫)** — the high-end anchor, close to a furniture museum in scope and positioning.
- **Huangchao (皇朝家私城)** — the mid-market volume floor.
- **Red Star Macalline (红星美凯龙)** — the national-chain flagship.

For a buyer, this matters practically: the five malls segment by price tier, so a sourcing trip can
be structured from Louvre (design/quality reference) down to Shunlian South (volume) in one corridor
rather than one undifferentiated market.

## The Zhejiang contrast: where the big firms live

Foshan's furniture industry is atomized by design of its history; the *scale* furniture companies —
firms with RMB 10 billion-plus output — are mostly in Zhejiang [1]. Foshan's single-firm ceiling of
~RMB 1 billion means the cluster competes on **market access and variety**, not on giant anchored
brands. Buyers should read this as the cluster's honest profile: a discovery market plus a
fragmented production tail, with limited smart-factory presence [1].

## The Foshan-Dongguan pan-home link

Foshan's furniture cluster is formally paired with Dongguan in the **Foshan-Dongguan "pan-home"
(泛家居) advanced-manufacturing cluster** — a cross-city policy construct that Dongguan's industry
bureau was still advancing in 2024 [3]. Dongguan contributes the world-class furniture-events side
(the 2024 World Furniture Cluster event, hosted by the Dongguan government and the China Furniture
Association, was again held there) [4]. The practical meaning: furniture sourcing in the Pearl
River Delta works as one Foshan-Dongguan corridor — Foshan for the market-plus-factory base,
Dongguan for events, design, and cross-category bundling.

*Inference:* the mechanism is "manufacturing + trading-city dual core." Foshan furniture is
*fragmented and labor-intensive* — its moat is *market density* (the 2 million m² Lecong floor) plus
*material co-location* (Shunde also anchors appliances), not automation or a champion brand. Longjiang
is the factory core; Lecong is the discovery-and-comparison floor.

## Data deep-dive

- **30,000+ firms** — 9,000 manufacturers + 20,000+ traders [1].
- **2 million+ m²** — Lecong Furniture City, 3,000+ dealers [1][2].
- **RMB 1 billion** — the single-firm output ceiling, signaling atomization [1].
- **Five anchor malls** along Foshan Avenue — Shunlian N/S, Louvre, Huangchao, Red Star Macalline [1].
- **RMB 10B+ furniture firms are mostly in Zhejiang** — not Foshan [1].
- **Foshan-Dongguan pan-home cluster** — cross-city pairing still under construction in 2024 [3].

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Foshan is the correct floor for **furniture sourcing**, with Lecong as the
discovery-and-comparison layer — you can physically compare 3,000 dealers in one place. But the
fragmentation cuts both ways: near-infinite choice, near-zero brand guarantees. The RMB 1B single-firm
ceiling means no scale anchor — verify factory capability per order. For buyers, the practical move is:
use Lecong for discovery, then qualify individual Longjiang factories. The structural insight is that
Foshan furniture competes on *market access*, not on automation or brand — a contrast to the
appliance cluster (Shunde) that shares the same district.

## What it makes

- **Sofas, office furniture, mattresses, home furniture.**
- **Knock-down (KD) furniture** for export.

## City context: Foshan's RMB 3 trillion industrial base

Foshan's furniture cluster sits inside a city whose above-scale industrial output has broken
**RMB 3 trillion** — Guangdong's #3 economy, China's #4 industrial city [5]. The city's
manufacturing is unusually self-sustaining: home appliances, furniture, and building materials
serve China's domestic consumer market directly, and Midea-class anchors give the economy
endogenous demand [5]. For the furniture cluster this means the domestic renovation and
home-furnishing cycle is the base load, with exports as the swing factor.

The city's 2022–2025 digitization plan explicitly targets the furniture segment: leading firms
are pushed to build industry-level industrial-internet platforms with supply-chain cloud
collaboration — search, inquiry, ordering, delivery in one chain — plus personalized
customization and flexible production, moving furniture manufacturing toward a service model
[6]. That policy push is the counterweight to the "limited smart factories" caveat: the
digitization frontier is being built, but so far it runs through the leading firms, not the
long tail.

## The export cycle: furniture is a swing export

Furniture is a volatile export category. China's furniture-and-parts exports grew **18.3%**
year-on-year in H1 2024 on recovering demand and cross-border e-commerce, then the 2025
full-year figure came in at **RMB 455.94 billion, down 5.6%**, with December down 9.8% [7][8].
The swing is instructive: the cluster's export volume moves with tariffs, freight, and
overseas housing cycles, while the Lecong domestic floor stays comparatively stable. Buyers
should read Foshan as a *domestic-floor, export-swing* cluster — not an export-first base.

## The pan-home trillion-yuan context

Foshan's furniture cluster is one slice of a much larger "pan-home" (泛家居) economy — the
city's cross-category home-products complex spanning ceramics, sanitary ware, appliances,
aluminum doors/windows, custom cabinetry, lighting, furniture, flooring, and plastic pipes:
14 categories at the brand-IP level alone, with **41 leading brand-IP enterprises** designated
by early 2024 [9]. The pan-home cluster passed **RMB 1 trillion in output value in 2020 and
2021 consecutively**, covering 26 sub-industries with what the city calls the country's most
complete home-furnishing chain, exporting to 100+ countries [9][10].

The practical meaning for furniture buyers: Foshan furniture is rarely sourced alone. The
pan-home structure means a single trip can resolve flooring, doors, lighting, sanitary ware,
and furniture against one supply web — the cross-category bundling that the Foshan-Dongguan
cluster designation formalizes [3][9]. Nationally, the furniture industry's above-scale firms
(7,459 of them) made **RMB 677.15 billion in 2024 revenue (+0.4%)** — a mature, slow-growth
industry where Foshan's market-density advantage counts more than category growth [11]. In
that environment, the digitization plan's flexible-production and cloud-collaboration goals
are the city's answer to the maturity trap: turning the trading floor into a data-coordinated
supply web rather than a pure bazaar [6].

## The honest caveats

- **Atomized sector.** No single firm >RMB 1B; per-factory quality varies.
- **Labor-intensive.** "智能化工厂较少" — limited smart factories.
- **Zhejiang holds the scale tier.** For RMB 10B-scale furniture groups, the sourcing map points
  north to Zhejiang, not Foshan.
- **Export volatility.** 2024 H1 +18.3% vs 2025 full-year -5.6% — plan around the swing [7][8].
- **Slow category growth.** National above-scale furniture revenue grew only +0.4% in 2024 [11].

## What this means for buyers

- **Furniture discovery + comparison** at Lecong (2M m²) — segment the trip by the five malls' price
  tiers.
- **Factory qualification** in Longjiang.
- **Pan-home bundling** — pair Foshan with Dongguan's furniture events and cross-category sourcing.
- **Cross-category resolution** — flooring, doors, lighting, sanitary ware, and furniture against
  one supply web in a single trip [9].
- **Digital frontier** — flexible production and cloud collaboration are rolling out through the
  leading firms; factor them into longer-horizon programs [6].
- **Caveats:** no scale anchor; verify per-factory capability.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Foshan is the right floor for furniture sourcing, with Lecong as the discovery layer — but fragmentation is the price of that convenience.*

Lecong lets you physically compare thousands of dealers in one place, which is rare and genuinely useful. The tradeoff is a fragmented factory base: you must verify the actual manufacturer behind any showroom, because the trading hub and the production base are not the same thing.

*This is my editorial judgment, not a verified fact — the sourced figures are above.*
