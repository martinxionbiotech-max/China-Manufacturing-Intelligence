# Shanghai Integrated Circuits: China's Foundry Core at Zhangjiang

Shanghai's integrated circuits cluster — centered on Zhangjiang in Pudong — is the thirteenth formation
mechanism in this project. It shares a "state-strategy + university talent" DNA with Wuhan Optics
Valley, but where Wuhan bends toward optics and memory, Shanghai bends toward the manufacturing core of
semiconductors: wafer fabrication.

## What the cluster is

Zhangjiang's 2021 IC revenue was **RMB 170.256 billion — 60%+ of Shanghai's total** — across ~500
upstream/downstream firms [1]. **7 of the world's top-10 chip designers** have a regional HQ or R&D
center in Zhangjiang, alongside **SMIC (中芯国际)**, **Hua Hong (华虹)**, AMEC, UNISOC, GalaxyCore, and
NVIDIA [1]. SMIC — founded 2000 by Zhang Rujing — is mainland China's largest contract chipmaker (2024
revenue US$8.0B) [2]; Hua Hong is the power/analog foundry leader [3]. The Shanghai IC Design Park
targets RMB 100B to drive Pudong's full-chain scale to RMB 400B by 2025 (80% of Shanghai) [1].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "state strategy + foundry core + full-stack density." Zhangjiang is a
*full-stack* cluster — design (7 of top-10), foundry (SMIC, Hua Hong), and equipment (AMEC) in one
science city. That vertical density is rare globally and is the structural reason Shanghai dominates
China's IC map.

## The full stack: design, foundry, equipment in one science city

Zhangjiang's distinguishing feature is that every layer of the chip value chain sits in one
district [1]:

- **Design** — UNISOC (mobile SoCs), GalaxyCore (image sensors), plus the regional HQs/R&D centers
  of 7 of the world's top-10 chip design firms; NVIDIA's presence anchors the foreign-design tier.
- **Foundry** — SMIC (mainland's largest contract chipmaker, US$8.0B revenue in 2024 [2]) and Hua
  Hong (power-semiconductor and analog foundry leader [3]) form the mature-node manufacturing core.
- **Equipment** — AMEC (etch equipment) sits in the same district, closing a design–manufacture–
  equipment loop that few clusters worldwide can match.

This loop matters practically: design teams, fab engineers, and equipment specialists are a taxi
ride apart, which compresses the iteration cycle for mature-node and specialty processes — the
segment where Zhangjiang is globally competitive.

## The policy spine: a first-batch national cluster in a three-pillar strategy

Zhangjiang's IC cluster was one of Shanghai's **first batch of three national advanced
manufacturing clusters** (integrated circuits, Zhangjiang biopharma, new-energy vehicles), with
two more added in 2024 for five total [4]. At the city level, IC is one of Shanghai's three
"pioneer industries" (集成电路、生物医药、人工智能) — the declared carriers of the city's
post-industrial growth [5]. Shanghai's first three quarters of 2025 GDP reached RMB 4.07 trillion
(+5.5%), and the city's 15th Five-Year industrial plan places new-generation electronic
information — AI phones, AI PCs, AI servers, and the chip chains behind them — among its six
emerging pillar clusters [5][6]. For the IC cluster this means one thing: its customers (AI
terminals, NEVs, smart manufacturing) are being scaled in the same city.

## Cross-cluster position

Within China's IC map, Shanghai anchors the **manufacturing pole**: mature-node foundry volume and
power/analog specialty processes. Shenzhen/Dongguan anchor design-and-application demand; Wuhan
anchors memory and optoelectronics; Shanghai's complement is the foundry floor itself. Within
Shanghai, the IC cluster's natural customers are the city's other clusters — the NEV cluster
(Tesla Gigafactory ecosystem) and the biopharma/AI tier — which makes Shanghai one of the few
places where chip supply and chip demand are co-located at national scale.

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Zhangjiang is the correct anchor for **chip-design partnerships and foundry access** — but
the strategic reality is a **geopolitical constraint**: advanced-node foundry access is export-
controlled, so "made in Zhangjiang" spans mature-node volume (accessible) and advanced-node aspiration
(restricted). For buyers, the practical split: mature-node wafers and power/analog chips (SMIC, Hua
Hong) are the accessible floor; advanced-node is restricted. The RMB 400B target is a *plan*, not a
result — discount against the RMB 170B (2021) realized. The full-stack density (design + foundry +
equipment) is the genuine differentiator.

## What it makes

- **Wafers, power chips, analog chips** (SMIC, Hua Hong).
- **IC design** — 7 of top-10 designers present.
- **Etch equipment** — AMEC, closing the design–manufacture–equipment loop.

## Market context: a recovering global cycle

The cluster's 2024-2026 expansion coincides with a global IC up-cycle. Worldwide integrated-
circuit market revenue reached **US$626.8 billion in 2024, up 19%** year-on-year [7]. China's
semiconductor market was about **RMB 1,756.7 billion in 2024**, with ICs ~78% of it [8]. The
substitution wave is visible in trade: 2023 imports fell to US$350.17 billion (-15.8%) and
exports to US$136.37 billion (-11.4%) — both down, but with 2024 Q1 chip output jumping 40%
year-on-year as domestic capacity ramped [9].

For Shanghai this means two things. First, the cluster's expansion (SMIC and Hua Hong capacity,
equipment and materials localisation) is riding a demand cycle, not just policy. Second, the
down-cycle of 2023 taught the cluster to sell into domestic substitution demand — the import
decline is the mirror image of local fab orders. The 19% global growth and the domestic
substitution wave are different engines; Shanghai benefits from both.

## Why buyers should watch talent density, not just fabs

Shanghai's IC cluster is embedded in the country's densest semiconductor talent pool — the
science-city layout (design, foundry, equipment co-located) plus the city's universities
produce the engineers that fabs and design houses both compete for [1][5]. Practical
consequence: for design-services, IP, and advanced-package sourcing, Shanghai offers the
widest vendor bench in China; for pure cost-down foundry work, the city's wage structure is
not the cheapest option in the country. Match the engagement type to the asset — high-skill
IC services here, commodity packaging elsewhere.

## The honest caveats

- **Export controls.** Advanced-node tooling and some design flows are subject to export
  control; buyers should assess compliance independently.
- **Report calibers.** Market-size figures are industry-association and research-house
  estimates, not official statistics.
- **Capacity claims.** Fab capacity additions are company-stated targets; utilisation is
  cycle-dependent.

- **Geopolitical constraint.** Advanced-node access is export-controlled; this cluster is the most
  exposed in China to US/export-control shifts — procurement decisions must be independently
  stress-tested against control regimes.
- **Target vs. realized.** RMB 400B (2025) target vs. RMB 170B (2021) realized.
- **City-level figures are broader than the cluster.** Shanghai's IC totals include design and
  packaging outside Zhangjiang; treat "60%+" as the Zhangjiang share of the whole.

## The AI-era demand pull

Shanghai's IC cluster is being pulled by demand from its own city: the 15th Five-Year plan
places AI phones, AI PCs, AI servers, and their chip chains among six emerging pillar
clusters, and the NEV cluster (Tesla ecosystem) consumes power chips at scale [5][6]. The
result is a rare co-location — chip supply and chip demand growing in the same
metropolitan economy — which insulates Shanghai's mature-node foundries from the
boom-bust of any single downstream sector. Buyers should read this as demand
*portfolio*: the cluster serves automotive, consumer-AI, industrial, and data-center
programs simultaneously.

## What this means for buyers

- **Mature-node foundry and power/analog chips** — the accessible floor.
- **Full-stack design + foundry + equipment density** — iteration cycles compressed.
- **Co-located demand** — NEV and AI-terminal programs in the same city buy the same chips.
- **Demand portfolio** — automotive, consumer-AI, industrial, and data-center programs run
  simultaneously, smoothing cluster utilization across cycles.
- **Caveats:** advanced-node restricted; discount forward-looking targets.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Zhangjiang is the correct anchor for chip-design partnerships and foundry access — but you must price in a geopolitical constraint that no sourcing decision can ignore.*

Advanced-node foundry access is externally constrained, so the practical, realistic use of Shanghai is design, packaging, and mature-node work. Buyers who assume Zhangjiang gives them leading-edge capacity will be disappointed; buyers who use it for design and mature-node integration will do well.
