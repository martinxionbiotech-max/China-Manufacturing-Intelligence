# Keqiao Textile Cluster: Where a Quarter of the World's Fabric Is Traded

Keqiao, a district of Shaoxing in Zhejiang, is China's answer to a single question: where does the
world buy its fabric? The answer is China Textile City — the world's largest textile distribution
center, where roughly a quarter of the globe's fabrics change hands and where nearly 40% of
China's dyeing and printing capacity sits [1][2].

For a fabric or garment buyer, Keqiao is the deepest single source of woven and knitted textiles,
dyeing, and functional fabrics in the world.

## What the cluster is

Keqiao is called the "international textile capital." The documented scale:

- China Textile City's market turnover passed **RMB 400 billion for the first time in 2024**
  (RMB 400.99B, +11.09%), reaching RMB 441.39B in 2025 [1][3].
- Roughly **a quarter of global fabrics** are traded here, sold to 192–206 countries [1][2].
- The district hosts **8,000+ textile enterprises** and ~140,000 workers, with above-scale textile
  output value of RMB 151.28 billion [3].
- Keqiao holds **~40% of China's dyeing and printing capacity**, covering nearly all fabric types
  [2][3].

## Origin: from "water cloth market" to China Textile City

Keqiao's textile trade began informally — peddlers sold local cloth from black-canopied boats along
the canals, forming a "water cloth market," then a riverside street market [4]. In 1988 the local
government built the country's first indoor textile market; in 1992 it was named China Textile
City, the first professional market in China to carry the "China" name [4].

*Inference:* this is the eighth formation mechanism in this project, and it is the cleanest
"market-plus-industry" loop among them. Unlike Yiwu — a pure trading hub whose goods mostly come
from outside — Keqiao is a **closed loop of local manufacturing and local market**: the market
drives the industry, and the industry feeds the market. China Textile City is the storefront;
8,000 local mills and 40% of national dyeing capacity are the factory floor behind it.

## The "largest dye vat in China" and its green turnaround

Keqiao's textile depth rests on dyeing and printing, an industry that was once a textbook example
of pollution. At its peak, scattered dyeing works across 14 towns produced over 18 billion meters
of dyed cloth a year — enough to circle the Earth 450 times — but earned only 8 fen (about 1 cent)
per meter while using 12.7 kg of water per meter [4][5].

From 2010, Keqiao did something rare in Chinese industry: it deliberately cut capacity. The number
of dyeing firms fell from 212 to 109, 64 were shut down, over 100 were curbed, and total capacity
was reduced by about 30%. The survivors were consolidated into the Lanyin Fashion Town, now
China's largest green dyeing base [4][5].

The result is a dyeing cluster that is both cleaner and higher-value: over 60% of equipment is
internationally advanced, water ratio halved from 1:10 to 1:5, and per-meter added value now
exceeds RMB 3 for export-grade products [4][5].

## From commodity to functional fabric

Keqiao is moving up the value chain from plain fabric to functional and technical textiles —
flame-retardant carbon fiber blends, recycled fibers made from waste plastic bottles and old
clothes, anti-transparent outdoor fabrics (UPF 500+), and AI-assisted digital printing that
controls pattern error within 0.1 mm [2][6]. The Jianhu Laboratory (Zhejiang Modern Textile
Innovation Center) has tackled over 160 R&D projects across fiber, dyeing, and future textiles [1].

This matters for buyers: Keqiao is no longer only the cheapest fabric; it is increasingly the
source for technical and sustainable textiles that meet brand compliance standards.

## The honest caveats

- **Export skew toward Belt-and-Road markets.** Over 60% of Keqiao's fabric exports go to
  Belt-and-Road countries, with strong positions in Africa and Southeast Asia; the premium
  US/EU share is a work in progress [2][4].
- **Dyeing is regulated and scrutinized.** Even after the green turnaround, environmental
  compliance in dyeing remains a buyer due-diligence item [5].
- **Cyclical and margin-sensitive.** Textile demand tracks global apparel cycles, and thin margins
  mean exchange-rate and freight swings bite quickly [6].

## What this means for buyers

- **Deepest fabric sourcing:** over 50,000 fabric types — woven, knitted, dyed, printed, home
  textiles — in one market with daily traffic of ~100,000 buyers.
- **Dyeing and printing at scale:** ~40% of China's capacity with international-standard equipment,
  suitable for mid-to-high-end and export-grade finishing.
- **Functional and sustainable textiles:** an emerging strength for brands needing
  flame-retardant, recycled, or outdoor-performance fabrics.
- **Caveats:** verify environmental compliance at the dyeing stage; for the highest-tier premium
  markets, confirm the specific supplier's certification track record rather than assuming
  cluster-wide readiness.
