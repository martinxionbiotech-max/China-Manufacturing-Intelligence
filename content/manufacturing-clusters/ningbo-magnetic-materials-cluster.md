# Ningbo Magnetic Materials: China's Rare-Earth Magnet Base

Ningbo — a sub-provincial city in northeast Zhejiang — is the eighteenth formation mechanism in this
project and the first materials-level cluster it examines. Its output is not a finished consumer good but
rare-earth permanent magnets (sintered and bonded NdFeB): the "industrial vitamins" inside EV motors,
wind turbines, and industrial automation.

## What the cluster is

Ningbo gathers **~22% of China's rare-earth permanent-magnet firms** and produces **40%+ of national
magnetic-material output** [1]. It is a national advanced manufacturing cluster (one of MIIT's 45), and
built the industry "from nothing" — Ningbo has **no rare-earth raw material** of its own, relying on
industrial foundations to create the magnet industry, launching the "Ningbo · Magnet Innovation Capital"
city-brand build in 2023 [1]. YunSheng (宁波韵升) posted **RMB 5.464B revenue** in 2025, with NdFeB
finished-product sales of **14,197 tonnes (+10.29%)** and NEV-application revenue of **RMB 2.552B** [2].

## The anchor firm's 2025: volume up, profit swinging hard

YunSheng's 2025 annual report is the cluster's most concrete data point [2]: revenue RMB 5.464 billion
(+8.39%), net profit RMB 331 million (+247.95%), NdFeB finished-product sales 14,197 tonnes (+10.29%),
and NEV-application revenue RMB 2.552 billion. Read the pair of numbers carefully: a single-digit
revenue gain alongside a near-3.5x profit swing says margins recovered sharply — typical of a
commodity-adjacent materials business exiting a down-cycle — rather than a volume boom. NEV magnets are
already the firm's largest single application segment, which is the cluster's growth thesis in one line.

## Market context: a US$20B+ magnet market with China at 85%

Global NdFeB production reached roughly **350,000 tonnes in 2024, with China contributing over 85%**;
industry research put the global NdFeB magnet market above **US$21.5 billion in 2025 (+18.6%)**, with
sintered NdFeB above 85% of the mix and bonded magnets growing ~12% in micro-motors and consumer
electronics [3][4]. Terminal demand concentrates in six domains — consumer electronics, NEVs, clean
energy, energy-saving elevators, energy-saving appliances, and emerging categories — with NEVs,
humanoid robots, and wind power the reported growth leaders for 2025 [5]. These are research-house
calibers, not official statistics, but the direction is consistent: magnet demand is decoupling from
generic manufacturing and tracking electrification.

## The rare-earth chain: ore in the north, magnets in the east

Ningbo's position only makes sense in the full chain geography. Baotou supplies the ore (Bayan Obo,
the world's largest rare-earth mine); Ganzhou holds the ionic-clay heavy-rare-earth end; Ningbo — with
no raw material of its own — sits at the **fabrication pole**, turning northern ore into finished
sintered and bonded magnets near the ports and motor/EV industries of the Yangtze Delta [1]. The
paradox is the lesson: magnet fabrication is a skill-and-capital moat, portable and defensible, while
the mine is a location-locked resource moat. Buyers therefore route by layer, not by "rare earth" in
general.

*Inference:* the mechanism is "materials-level + created-from-nothing + downstream application." Ningbo's
"无中生有" (created from nothing) is the key insight: magnet fabrication is *skill-and-capital*, not
*resource* — driven, which is why it clustered near ports/industry rather than the mine (Baotou). It is
the *fabrication* pole of China's rare-earth chain, downstream of Baotou's raw material.

## Original analysis

*Opinion:* Ningbo is the **finished-magnet anchor** of China's NdFeB chain — the geographic split is
clean: Baotou supplies the *ore* (Bayan Obo), Ningbo makes the *magnet*. For buyers, Ningbo is the
correct anchor for **finished NdFeB magnets**, especially NEV-grade (YunSheng's RMB 2.55B NEV revenue is
the signal). The "no raw material, 40% output" paradox is the structural lesson: magnet fabrication is a
*processing* moat, not a resource moat — meaning it is portable and defensible on skill, a different
strategic profile than Baotou's mine-locked position. The 2023 "Magnet Innovation Capital" brand push
signals an upgrade ambition toward high-performance grades.

## What it makes

- **NdFeB magnets** (sintered and bonded).
- **Motor magnets** and bonded magnets.
- **NEV traction-motor magnets** — the largest single application segment.

## The upstream regime: quotas, traceability, and the 2024 regulation

Ningbo's magnet economics are set by upstream policy, not local policy. The **Rare-Earth
Administrative Regulation effective October 2024** put rare-earth resources under state
ownership with total-volume control, full-process traceability, and dynamic quota allocation
[6]. In practice the quota system is the price-setting layer: the 2024 first-batch mining and
smelting-separation quotas were **135,000 tonnes and 127,000 tonnes**, allocated between the
China Rare Earth Group (Ganzhou-centric) and Northern Rare Earth (Baotou-centric) [7]. Global
rare-earth mine output was ~390,000 tonnes in 2024 (+3.7%), with China, the US, Myanmar, and
Australia the top four [8].

Magnets are the largest rare-earth demand segment: permanent magnets take **~26% of global
rare-earth consumption**, ahead of catalysts (~17%), hydrogen storage (~15%), and polishing
powder (~11%) — and NEVs are the segment's fastest-growing consumer [9]. For Ningbo buyers the
chain logic is: Baotou/Ganzhou quotas set ore supply, ore supply sets magnet material prices,
and Ningbo converts that material into finished magnets. The cluster's margins therefore
oscillate with quota policy as much as with downstream EV demand.

## Who buys Ningbo magnets: the six terminal domains

NdFeB demand concentrates in six terminal markets — consumer electronics, new-energy vehicles,
clean energy, emerging categories, energy-saving elevators, and energy-saving appliances —
with NEVs, energy-saving appliances, wind power, and industrial robots the fastest-growing
segments [5]. Sintered NdFeB holds 85%+ of the magnet mix, while bonded magnets grow ~12%
in micro-motors and consumer electronics [4].

For sourcing decisions this demand map matters because the grades differ: NEV traction motors
require high-coercivity, high-temperature grades that only the top magnet makers reliably
supply; consumer-electronics magnets are lower-spec and price-sensitive. Ningbo serves both
ends — which is why buyers should specify the *application grade*, not just "NdFeB."
YunSheng's RMB 2.55B NEV revenue shows where the cluster's premium is concentrated [2].
The same logic extends to the emerging categories: humanoid-robot and wind-power programs
pull the high-performance sintered grades, while bonded magnets ride the consumer-electronics
cycle [4][5]. For a buyer, the practical move is to name the application first, then map
it to the grade-and-maker tier — Ningbo's catalog spans the full range, but the tiers are
not interchangeable.

## The honest caveats

- **Company figures.** YunSheng data is company-reported.
- **Resource dependence.** Ningbo has no raw material; relies on Baotou/Ganzhou ore.
- **Report calibers.** Global market-size figures are industry-research reports with
  conflicting calibers (US$21.5B vs wider estimates); treat as directional.
- **Policy exposure.** Quota changes (2024 regulation) move magnet input prices
  independently of demand [6][7].
- **Grade specificity.** "NdFeB" spans commodity to high-coercivity NEV grades; specify the
  application grade in sourcing.

## What this means for buyers

- **Finished NdFeB magnets** (40%+ national output).
- **NEV-grade magnets** — the growth layer.
- **Electrification-linked demand** — motors, wind, and humanoid-robot programs are the
  visible demand drivers.
- **Quota sensitivity** — monitor rare-earth quota policy as a price signal for magnet
  sourcing [6].
- **Grade-by-tier sourcing** — name the application (NEV traction, wind, robot, consumer)
  first, then match it to the maker tier; the catalog spans commodity to high-coercivity
  grades, but tiers are not interchangeable [4][5].
- **Caveats:** resource dependence upstream; treat share figures as industry estimates.

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Ningbo is the finished-magnet anchor of China's NdFeB chain, and the geographic split is cleaner than most buyers realize: Baotou supplies the ore, Ningbo makes the magnet.*

Its "created-from-nothing" origin — skill-and-capital-intensive fabrication, not a resource endowment — is the key insight. For finished rare-earth magnets, Ningbo is the anchor; for raw material, you go back up the chain to Baotou and Ganzhou.
