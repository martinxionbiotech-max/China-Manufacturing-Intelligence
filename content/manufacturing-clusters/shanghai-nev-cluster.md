# Shanghai New Energy Vehicles: Tesla's Gigafactory and the Local Supply Chain

Shanghai's new-energy-vehicle cluster is the nineteenth formation mechanism in this project — and
the first driven by a wholly foreign-owned manufacturing superplant rather than private grassroots
entrepreneurs, a state platform, a national industrial base, or a trade hub. It is the clearest
case study in China of a foreign anchor company reshaping an entire city's industrial map.

## What the cluster is

Tesla's Shanghai Gigafactory — located in Nicheng town, Pudong (Lingang) — is the largest
foreign-invested manufacturing project in Shanghai's history, with total investment of RMB 50
billion [1]. Its ramp is extraordinary: in 2020, its first full year, it booked RMB 38+ billion in
industrial output value; in 2021 that jumped 247% to RMB 131.9 billion (17.3% of Shanghai's auto
industry); in 2022 it reached RMB 183.8 billion (22.7%); and in 2023 it hit RMB 230.6 billion —
29.9% of Shanghai's auto-industry output — with RMB 8.39 billion in tax [1].

Around the plant, a local supply chain has grown for batteries, electric controls, and components.
The factory exports about one-third of its vehicles overseas (nearly 30,000 in March 2026) and
produced its 6-millionth battery pack in August 2026 [1]. It has also begun producing the Megapack
grid-scale energy-storage system, serving Asia-Pacific and global markets [1].

*Inference:* the mechanism is "foreign superplant + local anchor + Yangtze-Delta supply chain." Tesla
brings technology, brand, and export channels; the surrounding Yangtze Delta brings low-cost
manufacturing and localized component supply. The result is a hybrid cluster distinct from the
OEM-based models of Shenzhen and Dongguan — Tesla is a wholly owned plant, not a subcontractor.

## What it makes

- **Electric vehicles:** Model 3 and Model Y for domestic sale and export.
- **EV battery packs:** 6 million packs produced as of August 2026.
- **Grid-scale storage:** Megapack systems serving Asia-Pacific and global markets.
