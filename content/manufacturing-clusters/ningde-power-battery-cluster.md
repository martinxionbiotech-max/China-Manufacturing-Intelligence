# Ningde Power Battery Cluster: How One Company Built the World's Battery Capital

Ningde is the third formation mechanism in this project — a single company, CATL, building the world's power-battery capital.

Ningde is the extreme case in China's manufacturing geography. Shenzhen formed through trading
density; Dongguan through brand spillover from Shenzhen. Ningde formed through a single company.
Twenty-five years ago it was an agricultural city — in 2000, secondary industry was only 28.8% of
its economy [1]. Today it is the world's largest producer of lithium batteries, and its flagship
company alone supplies roughly two in five electric-vehicle batteries on the planet.

For a battery buyer, Ningde matters for a different reason than Shenzhen or Dongguan: it is where
power-battery chemistry, scale, and cost leadership are concentrated at a level no other single
city matches.

## What the cluster is

Ningde's power battery cluster is Fujian province's only national advanced manufacturing cluster,
designated by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and the city holds the title
"China's New Energy Battery Capital" [2].

The scale is documented at the company and city level:

- **CATL (Contemporary Amperex Technology)**, founded in Ningde in 2011, held a **37.9% global
  power-battery market share in 2024** — first for eight consecutive years — and **36.5% of global
  energy-storage battery shipments** [3].
- In 2025 CATL's share rose further to **39.2%** (ninth year first), with energy-storage shipments
  at 30.4% of the global market [4].
- CATL's 2025 revenue was **RMB 423.7 billion** (up 17%), with net profit of RMB 72.2 billion
  (up 42%); it sold 661 GWh of lithium batteries, up 39% [4].
- At city level, Ningde's lithium battery and stainless steel clusters together passed **RMB 300
  billion and RMB 240 billion in output respectively in 2025** [5].

CATL's city footprint is the anchor: about 330 GWh of capacity installed or under construction in
Ningde, with another 170 GWh planned, and more than 90 upstream and downstream companies clustered
locally [5].

## The model: "chain leader + ecosystem"

Ningde's cluster was built through a deliberate, policy-backed mechanism. CATL's founding in 2011
created the anchor; the city then filled the supply chain around it, project by project [6].

The sequence is well documented: CATL had no local suppliers for key materials when it started, so
local government and the company jointly mapped each missing link — cathode and anode materials,
coated separators, electrolytes, components, and equipment — and recruited suppliers one at a
time [5][6]. By the mid-2010s the effect compounded; today roughly 60% of power-battery production
inputs are sourced locally [7].

*Inference:* This "chain leader + ecosystem" model is structurally different from Shenzhen
(market-driven) and Dongguan (brand spillover). It works because batteries are a high-capital,
high-scale product where one dominant buyer can single-handedly justify an entire local supply
chain. The trade-off is concentration risk — the city's industrial base is unusually sensitive to
one company's fortunes.

## The two-track battery base

Ningde hosts both halves of the battery business [8]:

- **ATL (Amperex Technology)** — consumer lithium cells. ATL moved its factory from Dongguan to
  Ningde in 2008 and supplies the cells behind much of the world's smartphones, drones, and
  tablets. This is the direct historical link to Dongguan's smart-terminal cluster.
- **CATL** — power and energy-storage batteries, spun out of ATL in 2011 specifically for electric
  vehicles.

The same city therefore covers consumer electronics batteries and automotive batteries — an
unusual combination that gives Ningde a diversified battery revenue base.

## Geography: the Fuding megabase

The cluster's physical footprint extends beyond CATL's Jiaocheng headquarters to a satellite
megabase [9]:

- **Jiaocheng District / Dongqiao Economic & Tech Development Zone** — CATL's HQ and core
  manufacturing, the "core zone" of the world's battery capital.
- **Fuding City** — the Fuding CATL base, CATL's largest single project globally: 120 GWh planned
  capacity and output above RMB 100 billion.
- **Xiapu County** — energy-storage projects, including one of China's largest grid-side
  electrochemical storage stations.

## What this means for buyers

- **EV power batteries:** Ningde/CATL is the world's single most concentrated source of power
  battery cells and packs, with scale, cost, and chemistry breadth (LFP, NCM, sodium-ion) that no
  single competitor matches.
- **Energy storage:** CATL leads global energy-storage shipments; Ningde has built an integrated
  storage ecosystem (cells → systems → grid applications).
- **Consumer cells:** ATL's presence makes Ningde a practical origin for consumer lithium cells,
  overlapping with Dongguan's terminal cluster.
- **Supply-chain localization:** high local input sourcing (~60%) shortens lead times for
  integrated battery programs.
- **Concentration caveat:** Ningde's dependence on CATL means a buyer's supply-chain strategy
  should treat the cluster as essentially a single-vendor ecosystem — diversification requires
  looking to other battery bases (e.g. Yibin, Sichuan, or CATL's other domestic plants).

## The Author's Take

*In my view, Ningde's chain-leader-plus-ecosystem model is structurally different from Shenzhen's market-driven or Dongguan's brand-spillover models, and it works because batteries are a high-capital, high-scale industry.*

CATL built a world battery capital essentially around one anchor. For power-battery sourcing, that concentration is a feature — deep, integrated supply — but it also means you are largely negotiating within one ecosystem.
